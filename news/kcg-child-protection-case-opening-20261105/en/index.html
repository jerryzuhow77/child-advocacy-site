More cases do not automatically mean more abuse

Kaohsiung City’s response to a debate over case-opening figures is verifiable. Whether services and safety outcomes are adequate still requires public evidence.

Source published 5 Nov 2026 · Page compiled 6 Nov 2026 · Kaohsiung City Government

A higher child-protection case-opening total in one city does not by itself establish more severe abuse, nor does it measure administrative success or failure. The meaningful questions are whether risk is recognized early, assessed appropriately and followed through until safety is stable.

Verification boundary | This page summarizes Kaohsiung City’s public statement of 5 November. It is an administrative response to a statistical dispute, not an independent outcome evaluation. It does not support conclusions about any individual case or locality’s actual safety results.
Verified publicationKaohsiung’s Social Affairs Bureau issued a 5 Nov statement arguing that total case openings should not be used alone to judge child protection.
Still needs disclosureCase definitions, risk tiers, repeat reports, response times, service continuity and child-safety outcomes.

What did the government say?

The city said child-protection cases span neglect, young children left alone, severe abuse, protective placement and deaths. Collapsing such different situations into one number, it said, loses the necessary context. Its stated focus is early risk recognition and active intervention, not simply making case openings smaller.

What can a total tell us—and not tell us?

Case-opening volume may signal a change in service demand or the threshold for recognizing risk. But a total alone cannot separate more reports, changed assessment criteria, population composition, repeat reporting or different risk patterns. It also cannot on its own prove timely intervention, child safety or adequate services.

  • Comparable definitions and denominators should be public before comparisons are made.
  • Risk tiering, initial-assessment and visit timeliness, cross-agency referral and completed follow-up matter.
  • Outcome assessment needs de-identified safety and service results, not a headline number alone.

The next question is not “whose figure is lower?”

Child protection should not aim to suppress case openings. What should be answerable is whether children needing support are recognized, handed to a responsible service, and not lost between systems. The statement starts a discussion; comparable, privacy-protective public data are still needed.
