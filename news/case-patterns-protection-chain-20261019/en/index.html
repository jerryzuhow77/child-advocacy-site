Questions the evidence should answer

Footage

Measure compliant retention, completed and refused access requests, and response time within a defined population.

Visits

Separate actual child contact, absence, refused access and lost contact. More visits alone do not establish safety.

Reports

Record awareness, reporting, receipt, classification and action. Immediate reporting is required; 24 hours is the outer limit, not a waiting period.[4]

Limits

This is a purposive qualitative comparison, not a national dataset. It cannot estimate prevalence, system failure rates or group liability.

Every record should answer: where is the child, are they safe, and who acts next?

A necessary boundary: these cases are not interchangeable

The Kaikai case, Xuanxuan and the preschool-footage issue involve different facts, people and procedural stages. This page does not replace a court’s judgment or use one case to decide responsibility in another. It compares recurring protection processes in public material: whether warnings are identified, records are verifiable, reports are carried through, and follow-up leaves a testable result.

Evidence boundary: Matters concerning individual criminal responsibility, ongoing investigations or cases without a public judgment are described only at their published procedural stage.

Three timelines, one protection chain

  1. A warning appears: Injury patterns, care changes, a child’s disclosure or footage may require further confirmation.
  2. Professional judgment and records: Visits, medical assessment, contact and risk decisions must be traceable to time, basis and action.
  3. Reporting and classification: People with a statutory duty who learn of a suspected child-protection concern must report within 24 hours; the authority must also classify it within 24 hours after receipt.
  4. Safety action and review: Preserving footage, cross-jurisdiction handover, placement or support is not the end; the next question is whether the child is safe.

Four testable system indicators

  1. Evidence preservation: Are original footage, visit and contact records subject to retention periods, access control and audit logs?
  2. Record verification: Can managers sample-check visit location, people present and content, including across systems?
  3. Reporting follow-through: Are the timing and accountable contact clear from a concern to classification, investigation and safety plan?
  4. Public outcomes: Can authorities publish de-identified statistics on coverage, retention, inspections, access and improvement?

Our view: Progress is not merely another form or camera. Every warning should lead to a protective action that is traceable, handed over and accountable.
