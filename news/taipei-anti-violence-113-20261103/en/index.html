Turning awareness into a usable route to help

On 3 November 2026, Taipei City’s Department of Social Welfare published a release about an anti-violence parade held with the Taipei Soroptimist International Club. The release presented prevention of violence against women and children and “113” as the public message.

The event and its stated message can be checked against the city’s primary release. It is not a case-handling notice or a statistical report on child abuse, reporting quality or safety-net outcomes.

A help route must be usable

Remembering a number is only a first step. If a child may be experiencing abuse, neglect, sexual exploitation or another protection risk, information must lead to safe reporting and support. The response still requires risk assessment, appropriate intervention and continuity of services.

Taiwan’s Ministry of Health and Welfare describes 113 as a round-the-clock route for consultation, reporting and referral. For immediate danger, call 110 or 119 first; where safe, provide as much information about people, events, time, place and circumstances as possible.
