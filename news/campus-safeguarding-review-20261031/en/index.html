Policy watch | After an indictment and detention order, who closes the safeguarding gaps?

On 30 October, the Taipei District Court ordered continued detention with communication restrictions. That is a procedural ruling, not a conviction. The safeguarding question is whether reporting, supervision and long-term support will change.

Published 31 Oct 2026 · procedure updated through 30 Oct

The judicial process: indicted and detained, not convicted

CNA reported on 30 October that, after indictment, the defendant was transferred to the Taipei District Court. Following a hearing, the court ordered three months of continued detention with communication restrictions. The reported grounds included risks relating to evidence or collusion and repeated offending.

Detention is a procedural measure. An indictment and sentencing request are not a finding of guilt. The facts, charges and responsibility remain for the court to determine from the evidence.

The administrative actions already made public

Taipei's Department of Education said it completed an administrative investigation after receiving the report, dismissed the teacher, imposed aggregate fines of NT$13.5 million and barred him from teaching for life. The city also described a cross-department contact point for student support. These are administrative actions, distinct from the criminal court's eventual judgment.
Public sanctions do not mean the safeguarding system has been repaired. The test is whether other children are seen earlier and whether support continues after a report.

Legislative scrutiny and the ministry's commitment

During legislative questioning on 29 October, legislator Kuo Yu-ching asked why risk had not been detected over an extended period. Education Minister Cheng Ying-yao promised to clarify the case and review campus-safety management with the local government; the K–12 Education Administration also requested registration in the unsuitable-teacher system.

This is scrutiny and a policy commitment, not a completed reform. The scope, measures, timetable and auditable results still need to be published.

What should be tracked next?

  1. Early warning: whether concerns from children, families and staff have a route that does not depend on a single supervisor.
  2. Contact boundaries: auditable rules for after-school activities, off-campus contact, closed spaces and one-to-one access.
  3. Digital evidence: preservation of devices, images and accounts without exposing children or recirculating abusive material.
  4. Agency hand-offs: named responsibility and traceable times across schools, education, police, social services and courts.
  5. Long-term support: sustained psychological, medical, legal and learning assistance based on need, not news attention.

These are the site's policy questions drawn from public information, not findings about undisclosed individual facts.

Privacy and reporting limits

This page omits the school's name, the teacher's name, images of children and details that could identify an individual child. Public discussion should focus on verifiable procedure and institutional responsibility while affected children receive trauma-informed, sustained support without publicity pressure.

Verification status

Verified: the 28 October indictment and disclosed education sanctions; the 29 October legislative questioning and ministerial commitment; the 30 October continued-detention order.
Not determined: guilt, the truth of each allegation and any final sentence. Those remain for the court.
