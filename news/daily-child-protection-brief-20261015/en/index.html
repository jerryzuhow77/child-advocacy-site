Low birth rates are not answered by subsidies alone

The Action Alliance on Basic Education and 72 civic groups have called for ten commitments covering stable teaching, learning support, after-school care, school safety, mental-health support, affordable early-childhood services, family support and child protection. The proposal asks local candidates to address the conditions families face every day.

Taiwan recorded 69,446 births in the first nine months of 2026. Beyond the demographic figure lies a practical question: when families need help, can education, social services, health services and community support respond in time?

Protection must start before harm

Effective child protection cannot wait for a major incident. Parenting support, respite, and help for families under high stress should be available at key transitions such as birth, school entry and adolescence. Reporting, home visits, health care, schools and social-service referrals must form a continuous safety net.

Make commitments measurable

A signature is not a result. Local governments should disclose budgets, staffing, timelines, service access and outcome indicators; after an election, they should explain what will be delivered in the first 100 days and report progress annually. This is how children, families and communities can assess whether a promise has become a service.

Our view: A child’s safety should not be an election-season slogan. Every child should be able to return home safely, learn with support and find help before a crisis becomes harm.
