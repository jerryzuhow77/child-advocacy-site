After discipline is made public: how does protection repair?

A disciplinary record brings individual responsibility back into view. Durable child protection also requires truthful records, reliable visits, cross-city hand-offs and supervisory support.

Media follow-up: 29 Oct 2026 · Official execution notice: 14 Oct 2026

Verification focus：Administrative professional discipline is distinct from criminal, civil and other review processes. This page relies only on public material and makes no inference about undisclosed facts.

2Administrative professional discipline

First, what has been verified?

The Ministry of Health and Welfare’s social-worker discipline database lists a Tainan social worker, Lin Jung-keng, with a revoked licence. An Executive Yuan Gazette notice dated 14 October published the execution order revoking the social-worker certificate. Media coverage on 29 October renewed public attention to that existing action.

CNA previously reported the disciplinary findings: during a child-protection assignment, case records were not completed as required, twice-monthly visits were not carried out as required, and inaccurate visit content was provided at case closure. These are professional findings addressed through the discipline process.

Discipline is not the last word

Licence revocation addresses professional accountability; it is neither a criminal judgment nor a complete explanation of every harm in a case. Criminal liability, medical facts and the responsibility of other agencies or people must remain tied to public court or competent-authority processes.

Nor should one person’s misconduct be used to presume that all front-line social workers have failed. A protection system needs auditable records, workable visit schedules, safe hand-offs across jurisdictions, supervision and sustainable caseloads. That repair work cannot be replaced by a single disciplinary decision.
