From the rally to the principals’ meeting: protection needs more than post-incident promises

Frontline dialogue must become accountable protection. A public response is a start; the real test is whether reporting, triage, evidence preservation and investigation leave verifiable results for children.

What has been verified

On 17 October, the “Stop child abuse, protect children” rally was held on Ketagalan Boulevard. Public reporting recorded discussion of SOPs for suspected cases, timely intervention after reporting, preschool footage rules, exit mechanisms and the responsibilities of competent authorities. These are advocacy and policy directions; they are not proof that a law or administrative measure has already taken effect.

The next day, Taipei’s mayor said he was willing to attend the next meeting of preschool principals after criticism about previous attendance. It is worth recording, but attending a meeting is only the beginning of dialogue; it cannot substitute for results in reporting, investigation and safety action.

Scope of this report: We do not present advocacy, public commitments or political commentary as an enacted system. Individual-case responsibility remains subject to the competent authority’s investigation and judicial process.

How to test the system

Under current rules, local competent authorities should generally complete triage within 24 hours after becoming aware of or receiving a report. Subsequent investigations include visits, safety assessments, relevant evidence and recommended action. Public oversight can return to four checkable questions:

  • Was triage and safety action completed within the required timeframe?
  • Were footage, records and other evidence preserved promptly?
  • Can parents receive information directly relevant to their child while privacy is protected?
  • After closure, are non-identifying process, timing and improvement results published?
Next step to followLocal government, councils and central authorities should each provide a publicly checkable timetable and responsibility chart. “A meeting was held” must not become a substitute for “the system improved.”

When immediate help is needed

If a child faces immediate danger, call 110 first. For suspected abuse, neglect or other maltreatment of a child or young person, call the 113 Protection Hotline.
