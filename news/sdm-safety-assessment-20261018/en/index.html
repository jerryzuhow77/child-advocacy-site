Safety assessment is not a form: who confirms a child is safe?

The Ministry page keeps SDM safety-assessment material available. The crucial question is whether an assessment leads to protective action.

Page update16 October 2026
Material update27 March 2026
VerificationNo new law or nationwide measure found

What is verified

The Ministry of Health and Welfare's page for Structured Decision Making (SDM) child-protection safety-assessment material showed a page update on 16 October. The listed material itself was updated on 27 March and remains available for download.

Verification limit: This confirms continuing access to official material. It does not establish a new law, nationwide implementation standard, or case outcome.

Assessment must lead to protection

A structured safety assessment is not merely another form. It gives frontline workers a consistent way to identify urgent danger, record the basis for a judgement, and make a report, placement or family-support referral where necessary. Relevant professionals must report suspected child-protection cases immediately, and no later than 24 hours.

The systems question

What should be tested next is whether the tool is actually used, whether results become a safety plan, and whether cross-professional handovers leave an auditable record. A child needs action that confirms safety, not only a completed field.

Our view: Assessment is not proof that a case is finished; it should be the threshold for protection to begin earlier.
