After an assessment, what support does a young person need?

A Kaohsiung Juvenile and Family Court notice reports professional training on high-risk assessment tools. The training and analysis are verified; no individual case, recidivism rate or intervention outcome follows from that fact.

What does the notice verify?

On 10 November, the Taiwan Kaohsiung Juvenile and Family Court announced that it held professional continuing education on 5 November concerning youth high-risk assessment results and recommendations. The notice says the session analysed results from SAVRY, used in 2025, and PCL:YV, used in 2026, and discussed follow-up intervention recommendations.

That verifies a completed training and an institutional notice. It does not publish personal information, case scores, sample size, intervention outcomes or an effectiveness evaluation. This page therefore does not treat a tool name or training session as proof that any young person has been labelled, predicted, or helped.

What can a tool assist with—and not replace?

The stated purpose is to deepen staff understanding of assessment results, statistical analysis and practice cases, and to make assessment, management and intervention more appropriate. In multidisciplinary discussion, information can help identify where support, follow-up or resource connections may be needed.

An assessment result is not a whole person, and it is not an automatic answer for a judgment, disposition or support plan. Living conditions, family support, education, community resources and the young person’s own views cannot be reduced to one score.

What should be tracked next?

Public scrutiny should address system safeguards rather than identifiable individual details: the tool’s purpose and limits, data protection, access to referral and support, and how an intervention’s benefit is evaluated. Earlier Judicial Yuan work on youth probation risk management also discussed tools, local analysis, family support, network resources and trauma-informed capacity.

Protection should lead a young person toward support—not turn assessment into a label.
