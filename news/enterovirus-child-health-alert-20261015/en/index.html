Enterovirus transmission risk remains: warning signs for young children

The CDC says transmission risk remains; a lower clinic count is not a reason to relax safeguards.

What is verified

Taiwan CDC said on 13 October that transmission risk continues in schools, after-school programmes, nurseries and indoor children’s play spaces. There have been seven confirmed severe enterovirus cases this year, including one death.

Everyday protection in care settings

Soap handwashing, ventilation, cleaning toys and high-touch surfaces, home rest when symptomatic, and non-stigmatising communication with families need to be routine and actionable.

Seek urgent care: For a child diagnosed with enterovirus, seek prompt hospital care for drowsiness, altered consciousness, poor activity, weakness or paralysis, muscle jerks, persistent vomiting, rapid breathing or fast heartbeat.

How to read the data

The 9,001 figure is service use, not infections. The seven cases are the year-to-date number of confirmed severe cases, not a measure of any individual facility’s risk. Child protection also includes timely care and practical leave, substitute-care, cleaning and communication support for families.
