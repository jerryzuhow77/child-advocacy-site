CHILD PROTECTION POLICY WEEKLY · 2026-11-22

Weekly policy report | No major new rule in force

This week’s review checks public updates on child protection, adoption services and the justice system. The conclusion is not that nothing is happening; it is that no material rule already in force has been confirmed that warrants an immediate rewrite of the site’s legal content.

What can be confirmed this week

No material new child-protection rule already in force was confirmed that requires an immediate rewrite of the site’s legal explanations, social-work responsibility analysis or case features. The system may still need change; however, a discussion, a refreshed webpage, or an incomplete reference is not enough to present a direction as an operative duty.

How the update was read

The MOHW Protection Services Department regulations page showed an update on 20 November. That is a useful follow-up signal, but a refreshed page is not by itself evidence that a rule has been introduced, amended or commenced. The items that can be checked this week remain existing rules and guidance.

A 5 November Judicial Yuan committee update concerns trauma-informed justice, limitation periods for child victims of sexual violence, and privacy protection in judgments. These issues matter for child-friendly justice, but they are ongoing work and institutional discussion—not newly effective rules for this reporting period.

The site’s three evidence labels

In forceA rule is described as operative only after the issuing authority, commencement date and scope can be checked.
In progressDrafts, meeting updates and policy directions are kept visible, but marked as not directly binding.
Needs confirmationA repost, a page timestamp or an item without its primary document does not become a site fact.

Effect on four-language site content

No mandatory legal-field correction has been identified this week for the Taiwan or Hong Kong sites. Articles discussing amendments, guidance review or cross-network cooperation should keep distinguishing proposals, policy direction and rules in force. That boundary prevents an advocacy goal from being mistaken for a current legal duty.

Next verification points

The next review will compare formal notices from MOHW, the Social and Family Affairs Administration, local authorities and the Judicial Yuan. We will look for promulgated text, a commencement date, concrete operational guidance, or an order that directly changes adoption or child-protection practice. If one appears, the next report will set out the old-versus-new difference, scope, and four-language update fields.
