72 hours is not the end: what carries protection forward?

When no new case can be safely and independently verified, we return to the current system: after emergency protection, how can safety assessment, court review and everyday support remain connected?

Published 2 November 2026 · Basis: current official public guidance and law

Verification boundary: This is an explanation of the current system, not reporting on a new individual case. A report is not a finding of abuse, and not every report leads to emergency placement.

01 · PROCESS

Keep the terms distinct: a report, emergency placement and continued placement are not the same

Where neglect, abuse or other harm is suspected, help can be sought through 113, online reporting or a local authority. After receiving information, the authority investigates and assesses safety. The law does not send every report to one predetermined outcome; it requires necessary measures in the child’s best interests.

A report is not a finding of abuse, and emergency placement is not the inevitable result of every report.

02 · SAFETY

Within 72 hours: secure safety first, without losing the assessment

The Act authorises protection, placement or other necessary measures where a child’s life, body or liberty faces immediate danger or a risk of it. Emergency placement must not exceed 72 hours. The authority must notify the court and police and notify parents or guardians; for a student, the school and education authority must also be notified and a record made.

03 · COURT REVIEW

After 72 hours: continued placement is for the court to review

If placement beyond 72 hours remains necessary for protection, the local authority may apply to the court for continued placement. One order lasts up to three months and may be extended if necessary. The implementing rules also require an assessment before the 72-hour period expires; if the reason has ended, the child should be returned to a parent, guardian or other actual carer.

Seventy-two hours is a deadline for emergency action, not the endpoint of responsibility.

04 · CONTINUITY

The real question: is there a gap between the order and everyday support?

Legal steps create authority and time limits, but do not automatically make a child’s daily life stable. The ongoing questions are whether medical, schooling and mental-health needs are arranged; whether the family receives usable support; whether care, contact and return plans are updated; and whether the child’s views are heard safely in line with age and development. These are accountability questions, not unverified allegations about any person or institution.

05 · HELP

If help is needed: secure safety first, then use the right route

For immediate danger, call 110 first. For suspected mistreatment of a child, advice or a report, call 113 or use the Ministry’s online routes. Share the location and what is known about timing, circumstances and safety risk; there is no need to collect or circulate identifiable images of a child.

CONFIRMED | SYSTEM BASIS

The 72-hour limit, court review for continued placement and three-month term are set out in current law and official process guidance.

DO NOT INFER | VERIFICATION BOUNDARY

A single report cannot establish abuse, and a legal provision cannot establish a particular family’s or organisation’s responsibility.

HELP ROUTE | ACT NOW

Immediate danger: 110. Suspected mistreatment, advice or reporting: 113, available 24 hours.
