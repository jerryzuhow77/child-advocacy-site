Protection tools | Before enrolment: three public safety checks

Public sanctions, reporting routes and case timelines can be checked. When warning signs appear, protect the child first, preserve what is known and use the right help channel.

Protect the child first; verify information second

Unexplained injuries, sudden school refusal, insomnia or fear should not be treated as proof from online claims. Put safety first: seek medical care where needed, record dates and what can be confirmed, preserve the child’s spontaneous words, and avoid repeated leading questions.

Three public safety routes before enrolment

01 · Public sanctionsCheck published sanction records for childcare centres, carers, early-childhood services and relevant staff as one risk signal.
02 · Correct reportingSuspected abuse, neglect, sexual violence or exploitation may be reported through 113 or eCare; call 110 first in immediate danger.
03 · Investigation timelineCases are triaged and classified within 24 hours; investigation reports are due in 4 days for urgent cases and 30 days for non-urgent cases.
Public information helps identify risk but cannot replace professional assessment of a child’s individual situation. No listed sanction does not prove no risk; a report does not establish wrongdoing.

System test: searchable, reachable, traceable

A report is not the endpoint. A protection system should make it possible to find records, reach the right reporting channel, and follow what comes next—safety assessment, medical care, placement or family support—without turning a child’s situation into speculation.
