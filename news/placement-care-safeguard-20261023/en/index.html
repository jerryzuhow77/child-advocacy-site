After placement, protection cannot leave a gap

A Taipei child in placement was reported as possibly having faced inappropriate treatment. The city says it activated medical care, reassigned the staff member and opened an inquiry; the facts remain to be established.

What is verified

CNA reported that a Taipei city councillor raised concerns on 21 October about a child placed by the Social Affairs Bureau who may have received inappropriate treatment. The bureau said it had activated medical care, reassigned the staff member involved and begun an investigation under the child-rights framework.

This is a public allegation and an administrative response to a possible incident. It is not a final finding. We do not identify the child, family or staff member, and do not treat an allegation as a proven fact.

Why follow-up matters

Placement is one protective measure, not the end of the duty to protect. A risk signal in alternative care calls for prompt health assessment, separation from risk, a complete inquiry, and an information process that carers can understand and scrutinise.

If a child may be at risk

For suspected inappropriate treatment, neglect or serious harm, Taiwan’s 113 Protection Hotline accepts reports and consultations around the clock. For immediate danger, call 110 first. Do not share identifying images or details online.
