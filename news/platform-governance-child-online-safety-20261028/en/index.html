From content regulation to platform governance

On 27 October, the Legislative Yuan’s Legal Affairs Bureau published a research paper, From Content Regulation to Platform Governance: Legal Research on Social-Media Protection for Children and Adolescents. It argues that Taiwan’s current approach is oriented chiefly to ex-post content filtering and self-regulation, while personalisation, algorithmic amplification and sticky interface design expose gaps in preventive protection and platform responsibility.

An age- and risk-layered direction

For children under 12, the paper suggests stronger age assurance, parental consent, protection-by-default and limits on late-night prompts and personalised recommendations. For users aged 12 to under 18, it suggests a balance of developing autonomy, an option to turn off addictive features, proportionate parental involvement and platform risk measures.

What is verified — and what is not

The publication and its proposed direction are verified. It is not a law, an adopted policy, or evidence that any named platform has taken these measures.

Verification status | Primary Legislative Yuan material verified; this is a research proposal, not an effective regulation.

Child protection view: make safety the default

The key questions cannot stop at whether prohibited material was removed. They include whether default settings protect young users, whether recommendations amplify risk, whether a young person can understand and disable high-engagement features, and whether complaint and remedy routes are genuinely accessible.
