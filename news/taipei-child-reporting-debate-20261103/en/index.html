Taipei mayoral debate: child-abuse reporting

The Kaikai case shows why a report must become timely protection—not merely a record.

This page summarizes candidates’ public debate statements on 31 October. It focuses on system design and does not decide individual liability or judicial outcomes.

Two answers: speed and existing measures

Shen Pao-yang placed confronting child abuse among his first-100-day priorities: once facts are established, abusive institutions should be suspended or lose their licences, and harmed children should be promptly supported. He questioned whether cases receive equally swift treatment.

Chiang Wan-an said every case of mistreatment is a mayoral responsibility. He cited a three-party joint-visit mechanism after the Kaikai case, suspension measures following reports, and unannounced inspections; he said urgent cases are tracked through cross-agency task forces.

The reporting chain matters more than a slogan

Both discussed accountability. The unanswered question is sharper: when a medical warning appears, is there a non-negotiable timeline for reporting, follow-up, medical confirmation and escalation of risk?

How soon must first contact and risk assessment occur?
When must missed care, lost contact or ongoing injury trigger escalation?
Who joins up information when health, social and police records diverge?
How are inaction or inaccurate records investigated and made visible?

Children need promises that can be checked

Children cannot vote, but they have a right to be seen in time and kept safe. The next public debate should ask for deadlines, responsible officials and published results—not only expressions of concern. The Kaikai case must not be forgotten.

Sources: 31 October 2026 Taipei mayoral television debate co-hosted by TVBS and SET; public reporting by CNA, PTS News and The Reporter.
