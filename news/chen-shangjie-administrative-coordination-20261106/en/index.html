Courtroom Arguments · Part III

Was Chen Shang-jie only an
“administrative coordinator”?

The defence sought clarification of the Wenshan home-based childcare service centre’s authority. The question is not only who had statutory authority, but also who held information about the child and what action was available.

When warning signs appeared repeatedly, could a social worker who coordinated placement, family contact, visits, and updates to the grandmother stand aside as an “administrative coordinator”?

Two arguments presented in court

  1. The defence argued that the childcare centre’s statutory supervisory authority over carers does not give Chen the same guarantor duty.
  2. The defence argued that the child remained under the case management of another social-welfare centre, and that Chen’s link with public agencies was administrative coordination only.

Work actually performed and information received

According to the records summarized here, Chen participated in adoption-placement services, carer arrangements, the childcare contract, family contact, visits, and tracking of the child’s physical and psychological condition. She was also the primary contact between the grandmother and the carer.

From September 2023: reports included swearing, head-banging, fear of clothes hangers, and physical symptoms.

25 September: a first visit recorded a large bruise on the forehead.

6–11 October: Chen asked about recovery and requested photos; later information indicated the injury had not fully healed.

20 November and 7–8 December: further bruising, weight loss, sparse hair and tooth loss were reported or observed; a fourth tooth was later reported lost.

Public question

These were not only discovered after the fact. Whether the actual work and information trail differ from the description “administrative coordination” remains a matter for evidence and judicial determination.
