Play is a right

The Taiwan Alliance for Future Playgrounds proposed a “playable city” agenda: map walkable play spaces, improve parks in extreme heat, and provide outdoor spaces for teenagers. CNA reported support from 30 civic groups and responses from nearly 200 local-election candidates. A response to an advocacy pledge is not evidence that a project has been delivered.

Three everyday barriers

A ten minute play circle asks whether children can reach usable play space on foot. Cooling parks address shade and heat. Teen outdoor space addresses an age group often left out. Article 31 of the Convention on the Rights of the Child recognizes children’s right to rest, leisure and age appropriate play.

What residents can check

Local governments can publish play-space access maps; identify shade, age appropriate and accessible design in new or renovated parks; and disclose budgets, completion dates, maintenance and youth participation. These are questions for public accountability, not claims that any locality has broken a pledge.
