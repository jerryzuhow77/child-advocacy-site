Mental health is not an extra: what comes after New Taipei’s resilience forum?

When support can connect early across school, family, community and services, children and young people are less likely to face pressure and crisis alone.

A forum is not a support network

On 29 October 2026, New Taipei City’s Department of Health announced the completion of its international child-and-youth mental-health forum. The city said its discussions covered adolescent resilience, peer support and psychological adjustment to digital communities, alongside coordination across health, education, social welfare and policing.

What can be verified is that the forum took place and that the city described these topics and existing measures. An event report alone cannot show that every child has adequate support or that a policy has produced measurable outcomes.

Make protection reachable before crisis

Child protection does not start only after a crisis. When emotional pressure, peer relationships, digital life and family stress overlap, the crucial questions are whether schools, families, communities and professional services can recognise needs, offer a trusted entry point and make a reliable referral.

The next questions are concrete: can children and young people in different circumstances reach help, are waits and resources adequate, do cross-network hand-offs hold, and how do service users assess the support? Those questions require later public data and independent evaluation; this forum cannot answer them on its own.
