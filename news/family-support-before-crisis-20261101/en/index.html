When family pressure becomes risk: support before a child falls through the gaps

A service analysis released today is a reminder: when income, care and resource pressures stack up, support delivered early can keep a child from being left in the gaps.

Published: 1 Nov 2026 · data basis: CWLF 2025 service analysis

Verification note: This is an NGO service analysis and advocacy update released on 1 November. Its 90.2% and 61.9% figures describe families in its 2025 service analysis—not nationwide child-protection or abuse prevalence.

On 1 November 2026, the Child Welfare League Foundation released a note on its Family Turnaround Project, drawing on its 2025 service analysis. It says 90.2% of families seeking help faced more than one difficulty, while 61.9% of service families had monthly shortfalls averaging about NT$3,000.

Those figures describe families reached by this organisation; they are not a Taiwan-wide household survey, child-protection caseload count, or abuse-prevalence rate. We do not recast a service analysis as government statistics or equate financial pressure with maltreatment. The practical signal is that income, care, health, early-intervention and education gaps can overlap.

Support before the gap: what is documented, and what is not

The organisation’s public service description lists family support and referral, financial or material assistance, parenting guidance, and in-home childcare consultation for families with children under six. These are services it describes providing; they do not mean every family receives the same assistance or establish the circumstances of any individual family.

Early support never replaces formal reporting when there is a protection concern. Taiwan’s Ministry of Health and Welfare lists the 113 Protection Hotline, online 113 consultation and “Care E” reporting for suspected neglect, physical or psychological abuse, sexual violence or sexual exploitation. Use emergency services first where there is immediate danger.
