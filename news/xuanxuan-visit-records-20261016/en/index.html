Xuanxuan case: what a false visit report exposes

A child had moved to another city, yet the case record still reported a visit in the original jurisdiction. The disciplinary ruling raises not only a question of professional misconduct, but whether cross-city protection can truly reach a child.

From 15 April 2024The social worker handled Xuanxuan’s child-protection case.
November 2025The child had moved with family to Taichung.
Decision, 7 September 2026The committee unanimously revoked the social worker certificate and practising licence.

What the disciplinary decision establishes

Information released through the Ministry of Health and Welfare’s disciplinary register says the case worker did not properly write case records, did not complete the required two visits per month, and supplied inaccurate visit information at a case-closure assessment meeting. The worker acknowledged professional errors. The committee found serious or repeated professional negligence and a breach of the social-work ethics code.

Language note: This page uses “inaccurate visit report/record” for the published disciplinary finding. Any separate criminal or other legal liability must be determined by the competent authorities.

A move that should have been visible

November 2025: Xuanxuan moved from Tainan to Taichung with family.

January 2026: A report still stated that a visit had been made in Tainan; later cross-city checking found it inconsistent with the actual residence.

14 February 2026: Xuanxuan died in a suspected abuse case. The gap between the visit report and residence information emerged after inter-city contact.

July and October 2026: The central government first published its systems review, then the disciplinary outcome.

Two visits a month are not a paperwork ritual

For a high-risk child-protection case, a visit is a basic opportunity to establish that a child is present and safe, and whether care conditions have worsened. Once a child has moved, confirming the residence, cross-jurisdictional assistance, transfer of responsibility and actual contact all need an auditable trail.

Systems cannot wait to reconcile records after a death

The ministry proposed stronger risk sensitivity, better cross-jurisdiction transitions and routine case audits. The next step must be measurable: when a move or loss of contact triggers assistance; who confirms real contact before and after handover; how missed visits escalate; and how audits detect inaccurate or empty records early.

Our view: Revoking professional credentials is necessary discipline, but it cannot replace system repair. Every record that says “visited” must correspond to a real act of seeing the child.
