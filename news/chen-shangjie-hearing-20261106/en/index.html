A full public gallery: child safety cannot be divided into “side dishes”

All 60 public-gallery seats were filled for the appeal hearing in the Chen Shang-jie case on 6 November. Thank you to everyone who attended and followed the proceedings.

This is a record of public advocacy and on-site impressions, not a court transcript. Facts, individual responsibility, and legal conclusions remain subject to official court information and final judgments.

Can a lunchbox be responsible only for its main dish?

Imagine a pork-rib lunchbox causes food poisoning and testing finds the problem in a side dish. Could the shop simply say it only guarantees the safety of the ribs?

A complete meal cannot disclaim its side dishes. Likewise, where a child’s life is at risk, titles and differing specialties should not pre-emptively divide every part of the care chain from scrutiny.

Public scrutiny is not a judicial verdict. It asks that information actually held, available measures, and the division of institutional work all be examined fully.
