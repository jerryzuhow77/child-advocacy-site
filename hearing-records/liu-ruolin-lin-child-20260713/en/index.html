Scope of this record:Compiled from public-hearing observation notes, witness accounts and observers’ summaries. Statements about conduct, conversations and relationships remain witness accounts or matters awaiting investigation.
01 · Childcare arrangements

Care arrangements for the Lin child

The observation summary records that the child was born in February 2023 and entered full-time care with Liu Ruolin at one month old. Care later included holidays; the family did not take the child home, and the father signed the childcare contract. Although the child originally lived in Tainan, the family arranged care in Taipei. The mother said the grandfather often travelled north alone to visit his grandson and did not allow the parents to accompany him.

02 · Photographs and video

Dispute over images of pulling a pacifier

The defence argued that Liu Ruolin sent photographs and videos of the child pulling a pacifier to show normal development, and that grabbing objects could demonstrate activity and development. The mother said she saw the material only after the separate fatal child abuse case involving the Liu sisters came to light. She disagreed with the care practices and had previously asked for a different caregiver, but could not arrange a change because her father-in-law paid the fees.

03 · The mother’s account

Visits, outings and family decisions

The mother said the Liu sisters followed only her father-in-law’s wishes on childcare and did not respect her decisions as the child’s mother, prompting her to leave the parents’ chat group. She could visit early on, but said the caregivers later repeatedly gave reasons to defer visits, leaving her almost unable to see the child from August or September 2023. She also said the two caregivers took the child out without first telling the parents, causing her to worry about safety.

04 · A statement requiring clarification

An unsettling remark attributed to Liu Caixuan

The mother alleged that Liu Caixuan had said, in translation, “Fortunately, your child is not in my care…” and said this frightened her. The full context, timing and meaning of the remark still require clarification through other evidence and witness testimony.

05 · Subsequent contact

Account of continued contact with the caregivers

The mother said that after the Liu sisters were detained in the separate fatal child abuse case, her father-in-law still took the family to the caregivers’ residence, went upstairs alone to talk, and even continued giving the caregivers a year-end bonus. This is a witness account recorded at the hearing and remains subject to checks against objective material.

06 · Subsidy dispute

Alleged proposal to continue subsidies under another person’s name

The mother alleged that her father-in-law suggested naming other people as childcare providers to continue applying for quasi-public childcare subsidies. She said the family ultimately did not follow this proposal and instead brought the child back to Tainan to care for him themselves. Whether any false claim occurred must be determined from applications and the competent authority’s investigation.

07 · Request for a medical examination

The mother’s wish to take the child to hospital

The mother said she had wanted to take the child to hospital for an injury examination, but her husband objected on the ground that they should not anger his father. The observation summary interprets this as indicating concern about the child’s physical condition. Family power, financial relationships and the actual decision-making process still require examination through case materials and testimony.

08 · Prior acquaintance

Did the grandfather already know Liu Ruolin?

In a statement taken after the incident, the mother reportedly said her father-in-law knew Liu Ruolin before the childcare arrangement. The child’s father, however, told the court that his father had not known her. These accounts differ and may require checks against communications, messages, contracts, financial records or other objective material.

09 · A potential key witness

Prosecution did not rule out summoning the grandfather

According to the observation record, the prosecutor said at the hearing that summoning the grandfather was not ruled out. Questions for investigation include why he often entered the caregivers’ residence alone, whether he noticed anything unusual, the reasons for subsequent visits and year-end bonuses, whether he opposed changing caregivers or an injury examination, and whether he proposed using another person’s name to continue receiving subsidies.

Editorial principles for checking evidence
  • If financial, personal or shared interests existed between the grandfather, caregivers and family members, the objectivity of their testimony should be examined.
  • Oral accounts should be checked alongside financial records, communications, conversations, childcare contracts, subsidy records, access records, photographs and video.
10 · Continuing examination

Questions for an overall assessment

  • Did the caregivers restrict parental visits or take the child out without consent?
  • Did Kaikai, the Pan child, the Cai child or the Lin child show unexplained injuries or abnormalities?
  • Why were the mother’s requests for a different caregiver or an injury examination obstructed?
  • What interactions and interests existed between the grandfather and the Liu sisters?
  • Did the parents’ chat group, photographs and videos present only selected fragments?

The issue extends beyond whether a single image can show “normal development”: the overall care, parental participation and objective records need to be checked against each other.
