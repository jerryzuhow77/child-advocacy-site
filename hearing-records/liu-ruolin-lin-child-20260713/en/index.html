How to read this record: The following is compiled from public-hearing observation notes, witness accounts and observers’ summaries. Statements about individuals’ conduct, conversations and relationships remain witness accounts or matters requiring investigation.

Source guide: witness accounts and defense arguments are attributed. Evidence-checking suggestions and questions are observers’ summaries. This page does not provide a list of facts established by the court.

01 · Childcare arrangements

Care arrangements for the Lin child

Source: witness account and observation summary; not a court finding.

The observation summary records that the child was born in February 2023 and entered full-day care with Liu Ruo-lin after the first month. Care later extended to holidays, when the family did not take the child home. The father signed the childcare contract. Although the child originally lived in Tainan, the family arranged care in Taipei. The mother said the grandfather frequently travelled north alone to visit his grandchild but would not let the parents accompany him.

02 · Photos and videos

Dispute over pacifier-pulling images

Defense argument / mother’s account

The defense argued that Liu Ruo-lin sent photos and videos of the child pulling a pacifier to show normal development, and that grabbing objects could indicate physical ability. The mother said she saw this material only after the separate fatal child-abuse case involving the Liu sisters came to light. She disagreed with the care practices and had requested another caregiver, but could not arrange the change because her father-in-law paid the fees.

03 · The mother’s account

Visits, outings and family decisions

Mother’s account

The mother said the Liu sisters followed only her father-in-law’s wishes on childcare and did not respect her decisions as the child’s mother; she later left the parents’ messaging group. She could visit initially, but said the caregivers later repeatedly put her off and that she could hardly see the child after August or September 2023. She also said the two caregivers took the child out without informing the parents beforehand, causing safety concerns.

04 · Conversation requiring clarification

An unsettling remark attributed to Liu Cai-xuan

Mother’s account

The mother alleged that Liu Cai-xuan said, “Fortunately, your child is not in my care…” and said this frightened her. The full context, timing and meaning of the remark still require clarification through other evidence and witness testimony.

05 · Subsequent interactions

Account of continued contact with the caregivers

Mother’s account

The mother said that after the Liu sisters were detained in a separate fatal child-abuse case, her father-in-law still took the whole family to the caregivers’ residence, went upstairs alone to talk and even gave them a year-end bonus. This is a witness account recorded at the hearing and awaits verification against objective material.

06 · Subsidy dispute

Alleged proposal to continue subsidies using another person’s name

Mother’s account

The mother alleged that her father-in-law proposed naming another person as the childcare provider to continue claiming quasi-public childcare subsidies. She said the family did not adopt this approach and instead took the child back to Tainan to care for the child themselves. Whether any false claim was involved must be checked against application records and the competent authority’s findings.

07 · Request for examination

The mother’s wish to seek a medical examination

Mother’s account / observers’ analysis

The mother said she had wanted to take the child to hospital for an injury examination, but her husband opposed it to avoid angering his father. The observers’ summary interprets this as showing concern about the child’s physical condition. Family power dynamics, financial relationships and the actual decision-making process still require examination using case records and testimony.

08 · Prior acquaintance

Whether the grandfather already knew Liu Ruo-lin

Mother’s and father’s accounts

The mother had stated in a post-incident interview record that her father-in-law knew Liu Ruo-lin before the childcare arrangement. The father, however, said in court that his father did not know her. These accounts conflict and may require verification through call records, messages, contracts, financial records or other objective material.

09 · Potentially important witness

Prosecution did not rule out calling the grandfather

Prosecution procedural statement / observers’ questions

According to the observation record, the prosecutor said in court that calling the grandfather was not ruled out. The observers list matters for further inquiry: why he often entered the caregivers’ home alone; whether he noticed anything unusual; why he continued visits and gave a year-end bonus after the incident; whether he opposed changing caregivers or an injury examination; and whether he proposed using another person’s name for subsidies.

Principles for checking evidence
  • If financial ties, personal obligations or shared interests existed among the grandfather, caregivers and family members, the objectivity of testimony should be examined.
  • Oral accounts should be checked alongside financial and communication records, conversations, childcare contracts, subsidy records, entry logs, photos and videos.
10 · Further inquiry

Questions for an overall review

Observers’ analysis and questions

  • Did the caregivers restrict parental visits or take the child out without consent?
  • Did Kai-kai and the Pan, Tsai and Lin children show unexplained injuries or other unusual signs?
  • Why were the mother’s requests to change caregivers or seek an injury examination blocked?
  • What interactions and interests existed between the grandfather and the Liu sisters?
  • Did the parents’ messaging group, photos and videos show only selected fragments?

The key question extends beyond whether an isolated image shows “normal development”: the overall care arrangements, parental involvement and objective records need to be checked against one another.
