01 · DUTY

What was she responsible for seeing?

A child-welfare or adoption visit is more than arriving, taking photographs and completing a form. The visitor must assess living conditions, quality of care and possible risk, then preserve those observations in a record that others can verify.

A visit record is not an administrative attachment. It may become the next decision-maker’s eyes.
02 · TWO VISITS

Two visits and disputed dates

According to the indictment, visits associated with June 30 and September 22 were allegedly recorded as July 12 and September 26. The relationship among the dates, photographs and actual visits is among the questions the proceedings must resolve.

This report presents public allegations and systemic questions; it does not prejudge individual criminal liability.

03 · 9/22 OR 9/26

September 22—or September 26?

Four days may sound brief. For a very young child unable to seek help, each day can contain a new condition or risk. The court must examine how the record was produced, when photographs were taken, relevant statements and other objective evidence.

04 · INSTITUTIONAL FACT

How a record becomes institutional fact

Once entered and approved, a visit record may be treated by later staff as established fact. If the first checkpoint is inaccurate, the error can travel through reporting, assessment and decision-making.

A child cannot correct the record. The system must therefore preserve truth that is traceable and verifiable.
05 · WARNING SIGNS

When statements, photographs and dates do not align

Conflicting dates, missing image metadata, unusually uniform narratives or abrupt changes in a child’s condition deserve verification. No single sign proves abuse; anomalies are reasons to look again.

Look once more. Verify uncertainty. An anomaly is not a verdict—it is a reason to activate protection.
06 · CHECKS

Who should detect record anomalies?

Visitors, supervisors, organizations and competent authorities each hold a layer of responsibility. Dates and original image data should be retained, edits logged, and supervision should test substance rather than merely check whether fields are complete.

07 · QUESTIONS FOR COURT

Questions the court must still answer

  • Why did the actual and recorded visit dates differ?
  • When, where and by whom were the photographs taken, and can originals be verified?
  • Are the explanations consistent with objective evidence?
  • What effect, if any, did the disputed record have on later protection decisions?
  • Have all objective and subjective elements required for criminal liability been proved?
