02 · TESTIMONY

Dr. Qiu Yan-nan’s testimony | Five reading points

The following is an editorial synthesis of Prison Watch’s record. The full text and questions and answers follow below.

01Firm opinion

Multiple forms of prolonged mistreatment

Drawing together witness accounts, physical evidence, audiovisual material and communications, the assessment concluded that Child A experienced prolonged, severe and multiple forms of abuse while in the defendants’ full-time care; sexual abuse was also strongly suspected.

02Forms of psychological abuse

Psychological abuse is not one facial expression

Isolation, threats, prolonged punitive standing, restriction of movement, restraint, indifference, failure to comfort, lack of toys and interaction, cold washing and sleep deprivation were assessed within the overall caregiving context.

03Diagnostic level

Retrospective psychiatric diagnosis

The assessment found at least “adjustment disorder with depression” and gave a diagnosis of “unspecified depressive disorder.” Reactive attachment disorder and post-traumatic stress disorder were described as “very likely to meet the criteria” because the available information was insufficient.

04Avoid reversing cause and effect

Self-injury claims must not reverse cause and effect

If severe self-injury truly occurred, then after excluding neurodevelopmental disorders and neurological disease, it would instead support prioritizing multiple abuse and neglect as explanations. Ordinary separation anxiety does not cause severe pathology such as the described head-banging.

05Limits of assessment

Shared awareness and assessment limits

The doctor could not completely separate what each defendant had done, but said some material indicated “shared awareness.” Attribution of responsibility for individual acts remains for the court to decide on all the evidence.

Method of interpretation

The doctor stressed that a conclusion cannot rest on one photograph of a “sad face.” Material from different times and sources must be compared. The observed state was “not a single event,” but an ongoing change.

03 · THREE-LAYER EVIDENCE

How the three doctors’ evidence reinforces one another

The three experts had different tasks. More precisely, they did not simply prove the same point three times. Each addressed a different question: what happened to the body, how death occurred, and what the child may have experienced before death.

Physical injuries

Dr. Lu Li | Child protection medicine

Using injury location and form, growth curves, nutritional status, and dental and genital injuries, he excluded ordinary accidents, self-soothing and simple picky eating as explanations. He also noted that the absence of visible external injury does not mean there is no deep injury.

Mechanism of death

Dr. Xu Zhuo-xian | Forensic morphology

Autopsy, histopathology and toxicology informed his assessment of shock from pooling of blood and tissue fluid in subcutaneous soft tissue, multiple traumatic bruises, and trauma from restraint, prolonged standing and beating. Malnutrition was listed as an aggravating factor.

Psychological trauma

Dr. Qiu Yan-nan | Child psychiatry

Using audiovisual material, testimony and communications across time, he retrospectively found at least adjustment disorder with depression. He explained that withdrawal, apathy, fear and allegations of severe self-injury must be understood within prolonged abuse and neglect.

CROSS-REFERENCE

Four issues: how the testimony connects, row by row

01 | Restraint, prolonged standing and edema

Lu Li: Ankle injuries were more consistent with restraint; toddlers do not naturally remain immobile for long periods.

Xu Zhuo-xian: Prolonged standing can cause fluid shifts and edema; restraint can cause extensive blood extravasation.

Qiu Yan-nan: Prolonged punitive standing, restraint and restricted movement form a context of both physical and psychological abuse.

Cross-reference | Clinical injuries, mechanisms of death and prolonged circumstances connect within the same pattern of conduct.

02 | Can “self-injury” explain the injuries?

Lu Li: Self-soothing ordinarily does not cause the recorded bruising, bleeding, deep lacerations or lost teeth.

Xu Zhuo-xian: Many injuries occurred before death; a general self-injury explanation cannot replace the mechanism-of-death findings.

Qiu Yan-nan: The information does not support autism, intellectual disability or neurological disease; severe abnormal self-injury should first raise consideration of mistreatment.

Cross-reference | The three specialties challenge a single self-injury explanation through injury patterns, death and psychiatric differential assessment.

03 | Nutrition, activity and apathy

Lu Li: Declining growth curves and malnutrition cannot be justified by picky eating alone.

Xu Zhuo-xian: Malnutrition aggravated shock and the mechanism of death.

Qiu Yan-nan: Quietness and slowness may reflect poor physical capacity, depression or social withdrawal; evidence across time must be considered together.

Cross-reference | Physical depletion affects physiological resilience and may also correspond to the activity and emotion seen in images.

04 | Not a single event

Lu Li: Old and new injuries and deteriorating nutrition indicate abnormalities spanning different times.

Xu Zhuo-xian: Multiple injuries sustained before death and systemic consequences show cumulative bodily effects.

Qiu Yan-nan: Expressions, movements and caregiving situations at different times show ongoing change.

Cross-reference | Together, the observations support reading the evidence as a continuing process, not isolated moments.
Evidentiary limits

Mutual reinforcement does not mean that all three doctors testified on identical matters or that individual responsibility follows directly. Psychiatric assessment, clinical judgment and forensic autopsy each have methodological limits. Final factual findings remain for the court on all the evidence.
