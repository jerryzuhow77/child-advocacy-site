03 · READING FRAME

Four reading threads | Distinguish statement types first

Defendants’ accounts

Background and motive

Income, debt, family interactions and work pressure are self-reported; check separately against documents and other witnesses.

Responsibility stance

Admissions, denials and changes

Admitting binding, denying offenses, claiming misunderstanding and admitting prior lies concern different scopes and times.

Post-offense conduct

Rituals, deletion and settlement

Religious rites, deleting records, family contact and financial preparation cannot be combined into a single “remorse.”

Procedural boundaries

Objections and blank answers

When the court stops a question on sentencing opinion, the defendant’s answer remains blank; counsel’s or the judge’s words do not fill it.

04 · LIU CAIXUAN

Caixuan’s testimony | Financial motives to accepting responsibility

73 Q&A pairs. This first organizes her courtroom account; original wording remains in the Q&A section.

Income and referrals

She said September–December 2023 income exceeded NT$50,000 monthly, her lifetime high. Fear of losing referrals and income contributed to incomplete requests for help or reporting.

Changing accounts

She admitted not telling the truth to police, prosecutors and Dr. Hsu’s assessment, citing fear of criminal responsibility and inability to face it.

Rituals and repair

She said she attended all 12 ritual days. NT$300,000 comprised NT$100,000 each from foundation payments, a Ruolin loan and a temple-head discount. Counsel contacted the family, who did not want settlement.

Responsibility statement

Under the presiding judge’s follow-up, “Sorry, I was wrong” became “I should say I caused it”; she said causing the child pain and death was her fault.

“I should say I caused it.”— DAY9 · Presiding judge questions Caixuan
Identify the scope and timing of each admission

An admission in court does not make every earlier statement true; a single remorse statement cannot determine responsibility or punishment. Follow-up context is retained.

05 · LIU RUOLIN

Ruolin’s testimony | Knowledge, deletion and continuing denial

29 Q&A pairs. She discussed rituals, assessment, LINE, family and work pressures while continuing to deny the offenses.

Injury awareness

She said she had seen only the head and feeding method, learned of whole-body injuries only when the doctor illustrated them, and regretted not asking her sister or watching the child more.

Deleting LINE

She admitted inappropriate content and deleting it herself, saying she learned not to delete records.

Role and pressure

She held childcare qualifications and was the registered person, but described in-law business/staffing pressures and denied necessarily leading.

Responsibility position

She said the basin incident led to mistaken accomplice allegations. Under prosecution questioning she admitted only negligent injury: “I really did not hit him.”

“Yes, I really did not hit him.”— DAY9 · Prosecutor questions Ruolin
Denying offenses and admitting deletion are separate questions

One answer cannot decide the other. Deleted content, intent, evidentiary weight and responsibility must be assessed by the court on all evidence.
