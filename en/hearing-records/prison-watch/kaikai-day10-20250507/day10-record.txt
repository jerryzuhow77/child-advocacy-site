Yet, given criminal law’s psychologically coercive effect on people and the purposive theory of punishment, criminal law provides standards directing conduct and undertakes protection of interests necessary for social life—that is, legal interests. Once a legal prohibition is established, violators should be punished. Punishment is retribution corresponding to an offender’s responsibility, but also aims at crime prevention.
