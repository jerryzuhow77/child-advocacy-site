02 · COURT TIMELINE

From morning submissions to the 15:14 conclusion

  1. Morning
    Prosecution sentencing arguments

    The prosecution addressed offense circumstances, life and conduct, post-offense attitude, reintegration and general deterrence. The presiding judge conveyed the citizen judges’ wish to avoid repeating arguments about the facts.

  2. 10:23
    Recess; court resumed at 10:39

    Citizen judges asked three questions: why the sentences differed, why Liu Ruo-lin’s range was 16–18 years, and where her first-stage sentence fell.

  3. Afterward
    Liu Cai-xuan’s defense sentencing arguments

    Counsel argued for 16–18 years, citing caregiving difficulties, specific care provided, systemic gaps, no prior convictions, post-offense attitude and prospects of reintegration.

  4. 13:41
    Proceedings resume · Prosecution clarification

    The prosecution described Liu Ruo-lin’s culpability as moderate to high, as a secondary caregiver whose participation included omissions. The first-stage upper limit was 18 years, with no reduction, and 18 years was expressly requested.

  5. Afternoon
    Liu Ruo-lin’s defense sentencing arguments

    Counsel used three stages to address life history, personality, emotional dependence, participation and reintegration, while expressly maintaining the not-guilty position.

  6. 15:02
    Additional prosecution and defense arguments

    The prosecution rejected using systemic and personal difficulties as justification. Liu Ruo-lin’s defense asked that the two defendants’ conduct patterns and degrees of participation be evaluated separately.

  7. 15:14
    Final statements · Hearing concluded

    Liu Cai-xuan apologized and said she would accept the punishment due; Liu Ruo-lin said she had nothing to state. The victim participant’s representative requested that the family’s absence during the apology be recorded.

03 · THREE-STAGE FRAME

Three sentencing stages | How the parties interpret the same factors

Stage 1

Offense circumstances and culpability-based sentence

Motive, provocation, methods, breaches of duty, participation and the relationship with the victim first define a culpability-based sentencing range.

The prosecution emphasized duration, suffering and general deterrence; the defenses cited caregiving difficulties and differences in participation respectively.
Stage 2

Life, conduct and intellectual background

Whether personal circumstances—life history, employment, family, no prior convictions and social functioning—justify adjustment within the first-stage range.

The prosecution saw no basis for reducing either sentence; the defenses cited long employment and life histories.
Stage 3

Post-offense attitude and reintegration

Rescue efforts, apologies, religious ceremonies, settlement, willingness to receive treatment, family support and reoffending risk must each be assessed, not merged into a single “remorse” label.

The court still assesses the material’s evidentiary weight and final sentence comprehensively under law.
04 · SENTENCING POSITIONS

Three parties’ sentencing positions | Who argued what

This is a structured guide to the submissions in court, not conclusions already accepted by the court.

PROSECUTOR

Prosecutor

Liu Cai-xuan: life imprisonment
Liu Ruo-lin: 18 years
  • Emphasized almost continuous conduct over 115 days and the child’s fear and suffering.
  • Argued that neither defendant’s life, conduct or intellectual factors left room for reduction.
  • Invoked special deterrence, general deterrence and maintaining trust in legal norms to support severe punishment.
  • Clarified the morning’s 16–18 years to 18 years in the afternoon.
LIU CAIXUAN DEFENSE

Liu Cai-xuan’s defense

Final recommendation: 16–18 years
  • Framed motive as a vicious cycle and loss of control amid caregiving difficulties, opposing a reduction to “enjoyment of abuse.”
  • Cited basic care, seeking help, no prior convictions, long-term employment and family responsibilities.
  • Cited religious ceremonies, admission of guilt, acknowledging mistakes, settlement contacts and rescue efforts for post-offense adjustment.
  • Identified adoption handover, training, oversight and support systems as matters for systemic review.
LIU RUOLIN DEFENSE

Liu Ruo-lin’s defense

Maintains not-guilty position
Alternative sentencing submissions
  • Asked for separate evaluation of the two defendants’ motives, conduct patterns and participation.
  • Used life history, suppression and compliance, high emotional needs and conflict avoidance to explain her responses.
  • Sought the statutory minimum at stage one and argued for high reintegration prospects at stage three.
  • The source first gives an alternative 10 years 6 months for the fatal offense, then 10 years 1 month; it lists 1 year 6 months for the nonfatal offense.
“What the defense said” is not “the defendant testified again”

Day 10 chiefly consists of lawyers’ arguments. Their references to assessments, life histories or Day 9 answers offer explanatory models for sentencing. Whether and how far to accept them, and how they relate to offenses and punishment, are for the court.
