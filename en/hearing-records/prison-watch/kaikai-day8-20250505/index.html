03 · SENTENCING FRAME

Three sentencing stages | Separate responsibility from personal information

Organized from the parties’ statements and judge’s reminders in the original record: a reading framework, not a sentencing formula invented by this page.

Stage 1

Offense circumstances and culpability-based sentence

Role in the conduct, danger or harm caused, victim-family attitudes, and the upper responsibility limit supported by the offense itself.

Future reintegration prospects must not be projected backwards into offense facts.
Stage 2

General circumstances and personal information

Living conditions, character and intellectual/educational level, and whether these have an evaluable connection with the offense.

Background information does not automatically mitigate responsibility.
Stage 3

Post-offense attitude, repair and reintegration

Treatment motivation, social support, reoffending risk, rehabilitation prospects, victim repair and other legally permissible factors.

Possible reintegration does not require reductions at the first two stages.
Two reminders during opening statements

Citizen judges had already received sentencing instructions. Parties should explain facts to be proved, not turn opening statements prematurely into argument or repeat abstract descriptions of the three stages.

04 · FAMILY VOICE

Grandmother’s testimony | Care, adoption and repair boundaries

The source heading and Q&A table spell the name differently, so only the family relationship is used here.

Care and adoption

The grandmother said she cared for A for about two months. Because his mother could not care for him, a social-affairs bureau recommendation and Child Welfare League Foundation referral led to adoption proceedings.

Pre-handover impressions

She described him as healthy, lively, mischievous and lovable; she began crying as she spoke about him.

Post-incident contact

Neither defendant contacted her. The foundation contacted her once; she refused. Defendants’ relatives discussed compensation and settlement, which she did not want.

Ancillary civil proceedings

She was unsure whether an ancillary civil action had been filed. The victim-participation representative stated in court that none had been filed at that point.

“I really blame myself; I truly had no way. No one should do such terrible things to such a small child.”— Testimony recorded in Prison Watch’s published DAY8 account
Contact, settlement, compensation and ancillary civil action are four different things

Contact does not mean the family accepted settlement. Communication through counsel does not mean a civil action was filed. These are presented separately without filling in the family’s procedural choices.

06 · PLAIN-LANGUAGE GLOSSARY

Assessment terms | How today’s source uses them

Symptom minimization

The expert explained that people may downplay symptoms on self-report scales and doctors flag this; it does not make every answer unreliable.

Moral disengagement

A psychological mechanism using different cognitions to avoid guilt over one’s own or others’ immoral conduct.

External controls

Environmental controls: parents collecting a child are one example. A closed environment may reduce outside intervention.

4Ps × biopsychosocial model

Predisposing, precipitating, perpetuating and protective factors are crossed with biological, psychological and social dimensions to identify risks and protections.

Risk–need–responsivity model

The source lists loss of childcare qualifications, religion and family support in discussing future risks, intervention needs and support conditions.

Reintegration potential

The prospect of returning to community life after intervention, not diminished responsibility or absence of risk in this case.
