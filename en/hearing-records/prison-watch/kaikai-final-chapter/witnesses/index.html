06

Three lost baby teeth: the abnormality was noticed, but was follow-up completed?

Account/record A

蔡函妤 testified that losing three baby teeth was inconsistent with grinding. At examination, the wounds had healed and there were oral ulcers, with no obvious grinding wear or jagged tooth fractures. Because external force, neurological conditions or other systemic disease remained possible, she recommended referral to a medical center. 黃聖心 likewise rejected grinding as an explanation.

Account/record B

劉彩萱 initially cited grinding, adding self-falling and self-collision only after the doctor rejected that explanation, and did not disclose that all three teeth fell out in one night. 陳尚潔 received the nanny’s retelling; the available accounts show no direct verification with the dentist, medical records or referral results.

Interpreting the evidence

Social workers need not make dental or internal-medicine diagnoses themselves, but can check whether referral advice was followed, obtain examination results, verify directly with medical providers, and make extra visits or statutory reports as warning signs accumulate—lost teeth, changing explanations, earlier bruises and postponed visits.

How should differing medical observations, police-interview recollections and courtroom review of clinic video be cross-read?

Account/record A

On the day of death, emergency physician 黃聖心 saw marked thinness and injuries to the face and multiple other areas, and considered injuries at unusual sites difficult to explain by ordinary falls or collisions.

Account/record B

At an approximately 15-minute initial consultation a month earlier, dentist 蔡函妤 saw three missing baby teeth with healed wounds. In court she initially said she saw no external injuries on exposed areas, while an earlier police record included details such as head bruises. Supplementary observer notes say medical staff also discussed injuries on the child when clinic footage was later reviewed in court.

Interpreting the evidence

The emergency examination on the day of death cannot be directly equated with the dental visit a month earlier. But 蔡函妤’s courtroom recollection, police statement and reviewed clinic video are three sets of evidence from the same visit, requiring item-by-item comparison with official inspection records, original footage, police materials, medical records and X-rays. Staff discussions in the footage cannot be ignored or expanded upon without the original content.

What can 24°C establish? Can it alone pinpoint the time of death?

Account/record A

黃聖心 testified that the child had no breathing or heartbeat before arrival, had a temperature of 24°C, and showed no oral signs supporting milk regurgitation; the emergency team did not accept the nanny’s explanation at the time.

Account/record B

Body temperature is affected by surroundings, clothing, build, measurement method and prehospital treatment; a single value cannot precisely determine the time of death without a complete forensic model.

Interpreting the evidence

It can challenge a “just discovered, just happened” narrative, but precise timing still requires communications, CCTV, ambulance records, postmortem cooling and other forensic evidence.
