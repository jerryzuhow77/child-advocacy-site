01 · HOW TO READ

First distinguish which layer of evidence is speaking

Ten days of public observer notes are not a judgment; administrative correction is not a criminal conviction. Six kinds of labels show how far each conclusion can and cannot go.

Court finding

Addressed by a judicial decision

Based on court press releases or decision summaries; first-instance and final judgments are labeled separately.

Witness testimony

A particular person’s observations

Represents only what that witness described within their temporal, spatial and perceptual limits.

Hearing / documentary record

Traceable points

Public descriptions of observer notes, contracts, photographs, visits or medical records.

Administrative investigation

Oversight or competent-authority investigation

Used for administrative responsibility and systemic shortcomings, not as a direct substitute for criminal findings.

Agency response

The agency concerned explains

Presented alongside Control Yuan findings, preserving differences without independently adjudicating them.

Cross-source inference

Cross-checking two or more sources

Inference steps and limits must be stated; it cannot masquerade as a direct finding in one document.

Still to be established

Sources remain incomplete

Public material cannot answer; missing documents or verification methods are listed.

Important boundaries

劉彩萱 and 劉若琳’s criminal liability is final. The Control Yuan corrected administrative failings by the Ministry of Health and Welfare and New Taipei and Taipei governments; the Ministry investigated CWLF’s organizational-process shortcomings. 陳尚潔’s separate case currently has only a nonfinal first-instance judgment. These four responsibility lines interrelate but differ in legal character, defendants and standards of proof.

Citation and update rules

Major conclusions must be traceable to official documents, judgments, pages or hearing points. Cross-source inferences are labeled separately and must not impersonate direct findings from one source.

The Control Yuan anonymized names but recorded “the same social worker again” for the second procedure. FIRST-INSTANCE FINDINGS · NOT FINAL

Six first-instance failures: not “not seeing,” but not verifying fully after seeing

Taipei District Court judgment 114年度訴字第51號 found 陳尚潔 guilty of negligent homicide and imposed two years’ imprisonment, acquitting her of document forgery. The case remains on appeal; the following are only first-instance findings, not final facts.

01No demand for objective support

The nanny attributed self-collision, grinding, fear and other abnormalities to the child or former nanny without photographs, video or other material. The court found corroboration was still not requested.

02No investigation of conflicting accounts

Abnormalities all arose after the move to the 劉 home, yet the nanny’s account was accepted one-sidedly and blame assigned to the child or earlier care, without checking with the former nanny.

03No confirmation of medical treatment

After repeated fevers, allergies, injuries and tooth loss, complete medical care was not urged, nor was appropriate treatment of injuries actually verified.

04No increased or unannounced visits

Visit frequency was not increased, unannounced visits and detailed daily records were not required, and examinations, photos and past visit information were not compared item by item.

05Visits allowed to be postponed repeatedly

After postponements citing power outages or other children’s illness, no deadline for seeing the child again or alternative check was established.

06No report or rescue initiated

The court considered that active verification, appropriate follow-up or reporting for intervention had a very high chance of preventing death, and thus found adequate causation between the omission and death.

The judgment’s core standard

The court assessed negligence as failure to exercise care that was required and possible. It stressed that guarantor status arose from 陳尚潔’s specific powers, information and division of duties in this case, not merely her job title and not a general conviction of all social workers.

WHY CAN A STARVING CHILD LOOK BLOATED?

Why could a severely wasted child’s abdomen look distended at one point?

“Looks like a famine child” is a visual analogy, not a diagnosis. Photos show different stages on different dates; one image cannot fix the entire illness course to one appearance.

Courtroom observations in this case

December 3: belly still distended; later: more wasted

Dr. 呂立 said December 3 photos already showed malnutrition: “belly still distended, face smaller, later it will sink in.” The same review described clear ribs later and loss of abdominal distension, showing appearance changes as neglect and fluid status worsen.

General medical mechanism

Low protein and oncotic pressure may move fluid outside vessels

Severe acute malnutrition can involve bilateral pitting edema. Protein/albumin deficiency may lower intravascular oncotic pressure, moving fluid into tissue or the abdomen. Edema, ascites and muscle/fat loss may make the belly relatively prominent. WHO also notes higher mortality risk in malnourished children with severe edema.

Not established in this case

Photos alone cannot diagnose kwashiorkor or ascites

DAY5 says the earlier hospital did not test albumin. Public hearings do not confirm one cause through full abdominal ultrasound, albumin, liver/kidney function and ascitic-fluid analysis. Gas, stool, posture, enlarged organs or fluid may affect appearance; one photo is not diagnostic.

What can be said in this case

Public testimony supports the dangerous mechanism of chronic severe malnutrition plus generalized edema/fluid shifts and records changing abdominal appearance. It cannot precisely attribute distension to albumin deficiency, ascites or a specific malnutrition subtype.

07 · HOW THE EVIDENCE FORMED ACROSS TEN DAYS

How evidence developed over ten trial days

DAY1–DAY10 are not ten equivalent sets of witness testimony. Procedural foundations, everyday-life witnesses, medical assessments, defendant statements and sentencing submissions accumulated in layers. Choose reading depth by available time, then trace issues back to each day's source.

Legal explanation

Four legal tests before ten days of material enter criminal adjudication

Appearance in court does not mean judicial acceptance. Materials may reinforce each other, but quantity cannot conceal evidentiary gaps. This explains our reading method, not the court's reasons in an individual case.

01 | Admissibility

Can it form a basis for judgment?

Criminal facts generally require admissible, lawfully examined evidence. Charges, counsel's arguments, media reports and questions do not automatically become evidence merely by being mentioned in court.

Applied here:DAY1 opening statements and DAY10 sentencing submissions must be labeled separately from witness answers, expert reports, documents and judicial findings.

02 | Weight and evidentiary chain

How much can it prove?

Courts may combine direct and circumstantial evidence, but evaluation must respect experience and logic. A single photo, bloodstain location or ‘I did not see it’ has limits and must be checked against timelines, medicine and communications.

Applied here:Cross-day comparison shows support, differences and gaps; it does not turn one clue into a complete scenario.

03 | Presumption of innocence and proof threshold

Do not fill in conclusions while reasonable doubt remains

Defendants are presumed innocent until guilt is final; prosecutors bear the burden of proving charged facts. Conviction requires certainty beyond reasonable doubt, not defendants proving their innocence.

Applied here:‘Still unresolved’ preserves what public materials cannot answer; it does not fill gaps for either side.

04 | Offense facts and sentencing material

Determine the offense first, then assess sentence

Life history, post-offense attitude, compensation, rehabilitation prospects and defense sentencing views mainly inform punishment; they cannot replace proof of offense elements and causation.

Applied here:DAY8 sentencing assessments, DAY9 defendant questioning and DAY10 sentencing arguments revisit existing evidence, not create new offense facts.

11 · THE GREY ZONES

Eight gray areas turn ‘someone responsible’ into ‘no one fully responsible’

Not slogans but structural risks emerging from cross-checking testimony, correction and agency responses.

01

Childcare contract, placement in substance

The child lived away from the guardian 24 hours daily without equivalent foster oversight/support.

02

Home jurisdiction, care jurisdiction and agency separated

New Taipei held family, Taipei supervised nannies, CWLF held daily information, central government regulated agencies—no single safety lead.

03

Confidentiality becomes an information bottleneck

Guardian and New Taipei workers lacked direct nanny address/phone access; a sole reporting route removes second verification.

04

Completed visit ≠ completed verification

Attendance, forms and frequency can coexist with no diary, photo, injury, weight or location checks.

05

Partnership trust reduced suspicion

The Control Yuan records prior cooperation led workers not to particularly doubt the nanny. If smooth cooperation is rewarded, warnings can be explained as adjustment.

06

Assignments and income may incentivize silence

Defendants spoke of losing assignments/income. This does not prove institutional pressure, but honest reporting must be protected from retaliation.

07

Care transitions lack verifiable continuous medical history

Former nanny, guardian, worker and successor each hold fragments; without shared forms and receipt confirmation no one has the full growth trajectory.

08

Medical anomalies may not enter the safety network

When tooth loss mismatches age/explanation, absent abuse-identification, inquiry and reporting support can disable the last outside entry point.

Chapter 2 is not the end: six minimum safety principles

  1. One child, one nontransferable public lead.Referral/outsourcing does not end contact, safety verification or network-convening duties.
  2. Regulate 24-hour care by substance, not contract label.Long-term life away from guardians should trigger placement-level protection.
  3. Use firm warning thresholds, not one person's feelings.Unexplained injury, rapid weight loss, multiple lost teeth or postponed visits should automatically trigger joint visits and medical assessment.
  4. Visits must verify, not just ask.Cross-check the child, growth, diaries, photos, medical records and actual locations.
  5. Handover must be traceable.Prior/next caregivers and lead jointly sign care summaries, health/special needs and receipt/understanding confirmation.
  6. Reform outcomes must be public.Regularly publish refusals, postponements, missed visits, warning-to-response time and independent audits.
