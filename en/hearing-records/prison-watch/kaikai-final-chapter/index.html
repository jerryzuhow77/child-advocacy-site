What Chapter Two examines

  1. Two adoption-referral procedures — not two completed adoptions.
  2. The child’s care path alongside court records and Control Yuan findings.
  3. Where dental and medical warnings stopped, and which institutions were responsible for follow-up.
  4. A visible boundary between officially established findings and questions that still require primary records.

Different-case growth-record comparison

During the hearing, after prosecutors asked about Kaikai’s growth assessment, the commissioned judge introduced records from another child for whom Chen Shang-jie was responsible and made the direct comparison. The comparison is presented as a courtroom question about follow-up and record quality—not as a final judicial finding that unequal treatment has been established.

Evidence boundary: This comparison may help assess the consistency of care and documentation, but it cannot by itself establish causation, intent, or criminal liability.
