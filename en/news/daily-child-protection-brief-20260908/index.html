Invisible before reporting.
Waiting after.

Two cases test one chain: seeing warnings, accumulating risk, and delivering safety.

Bottom line: Measure more than report volumes: who saw a warning, when risk escalated, and why safety action was—or was not—taken.

Case 1 | The blind spot before fatal abuse

Audit source verified

For each year from 2022 to 2024, 61.90%–92.86% of children who died from abuse at home had no network report or protection/vulnerable-family service record in the prior year. Child-protection reports rose from 46,105 in 2020 to 62,314 in 2024; among some cases reported again within three months of closure, roughly 30–40% were reopened. “About 70%” is only media shorthand; no record does not prove no professional ever encountered the child.

Case 2 | Waiting for safety after reporting

Media report; official file pending

Reporting describes a Taipei ten-year-old subjected to prolonged sexual abuse and a substantial delay before safe placement. Dates, assessments, reviews and legal action remain unverified; “240 days” is not treated as an official finding or proof of individual liability.

Added to Early Warnings and the Twelve Doors

  • Record every contact: connect education, health, police, childcare and social-service signals.
  • Do not reset risk: repeated injuries, abuse or obstruction must accumulate.
  • Deadlines for safety: time-limit review, unannounced visits, medical assessment and emergency placement.
  • Audit inaction: record reasons, a safety plan and review date.

Comparison

No report/service entry and reported but not made safe promptly are distinct failures and need separate measures.
