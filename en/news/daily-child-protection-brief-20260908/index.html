Invisible before reporting.
Waiting after.

Two cases test one chain: seeing warnings, accumulating risk, and delivering safety.

Bottom line: Measure more than report volumes: who saw a warning, when risk escalated, and why safety action was—or was not—taken.

Case 1 | The blind spot before fatal abuse

Audit source verified

Across 2022–2024, 65 children died from abuse at home; 46 had no network report or protection/vulnerable-family service record in the prior year—70.77% across the three years. The annual figures were 13/14 (92.86%) in 2022, 13/21 (61.90%) in 2023 and 20/30 (66.67%) in 2024. Child-protection reports rose from 46,105 in 2020 to 62,314 in 2024; among some cases reported again within three months of closure, roughly 30–40% were reopened. The 65 deaths comprised 27 involving severe abuse, 14 severe neglect and 24 parental murder-suicide. These group-level patterns cannot establish the cause of, or legal responsibility for, an individual case.

Case 2 | Waiting for safety after reporting

Media report; official file pending

Public reporting says a ten-year-old in Taipei was allegedly molested at home by a man connected to her mother. After the case was reported in late December 2025, the city used a safety plan and continued services but did not complete out-of-home placement for about eight months. Judicial protective placement began after a reassessment in August 2026. “240 days” describes the report-to-placement interval, not 240 days of continuous abuse.

Legal and evidentiary boundary: placement can include relatives, a suitable third party, foster care or an institution. Without the full SDM forms, case notes, meeting records and court documents, individual legal responsibility cannot be inferred.
CNA case summary ↗

Added to Early Warnings and the Twelve Doors

  • Record every contact: connect education, health, police, childcare and social-service signals.
  • Do not reset risk: repeated injuries, abuse or obstruction must accumulate.
  • Deadlines for safety: time-limit review, unannounced visits, medical assessment and emergency placement.
  • Audit inaction: record reasons, a safety plan and review date.

The audit records the ministry's response: review major cases, require local governments to repeat safety and family-function assessments before closure, and improve cross-agency information integration. Follow-up should track public timelines, review quality and child-safety outcomes—not merely the names of measures.

Comparison

No report/service entry and reported but not made safe promptly are distinct failures and need separate measures.
