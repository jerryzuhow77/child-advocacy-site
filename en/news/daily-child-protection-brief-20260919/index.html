Question the dentist—what about a social worker’s reporting duty?

When tooth loss, injuries and a caregiver’s account do not align, how should the protection system respond?

Key point

Medical recognition deserves scrutiny. But once a frontline worker holds information that materially raises risk, verification, documentation, escalation and reporting cannot disappear behind job titles.

The Chen Shangjie case remains in second-instance proceedings. This article does not predetermine criminal responsibility; it examines the child-protection process through the public hearing timeline and reporting.

Timeline

Three dates, three questions that cannot be skipped

19 Nov 2023The caregiver reportedly told the social worker that the child had “ground away three teeth.” Sudden tooth loss or oral injury should trigger questions about cause, medical assessment and safety.
23 Nov 2023Public hearing reporting cited dental testimony that the oral wounds had healed, that grinding was not considered the cause, and that further hospital examination was advised.
25 Nov 2023The social worker learned the child had seen a dentist. The issue is not merely whether a visit occurred, but whether advice was verified, referral completion tracked and risk escalated.
Process test

“Saw a dentist” is not the end of risk assessment

For a high-risk child, a caregiver’s one-sided statement should not close the file. A protective response needs to ask what the clinician found, whether referral was recommended, who confirmed completion, whether new injuries or other changes appeared, and whether the caregiver’s account matched independently verifiable information.

This does not ask social workers to diagnose. It asks workers responsible for matching, visits or follow-up to use the information they hold for verification and reporting rather than simply relay it.

Not either/or

Medical recognition and social-work reporting can both be examined

HealthcareClinical assessment, referral and statutory reporting must be assessed under professional standards and legal duties.
Social workDocumentation, verification, enhanced visits, inter-agency contact and reporting must be assessed against the role and evidence.
The systemTracking, review and escalation across healthcare, care services, contracted bodies and authorities determine whether warnings vanish again.
Guardrails

Make the next warning actionable

  1. Close the loop on injuries: record cause, medical outcome, referral advice and completion; escalate unfinished items.
  2. Cross-check caregiver accounts for serious injury, tooth loss or repeated illness.
  3. Use clear thresholds: reasonable concern and multiple warnings should trigger protection assessment.
  4. Require auditable supervision: every decision not to report or escalate needs a reason, reviewer and deadline.
