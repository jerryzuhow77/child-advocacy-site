When the rules are unsettled, protect the child's best interests first

A US cross-state surrogacy and neonatal-care dispute and Hungary's adoption reform announcement: turning “best interests” into auditable procedure.

AI autumn flower-and-bird ink-wash concept; not an event photograph or a Qi Baishi original.

Today's two international developments have no final answer, but they expose the same institutional question: while parentage, jurisdiction or policy rules remain disputed, who must ensure that a child's medical care, caregiving and placement are not delayed? The US matter is an emergency stay application before the Supreme Court; Hungary's is a government policy announcement. Neither is a final judgment or enacted reform. Both test whether “the child's best interests” come with independent representation, deadlines, reasons and external review.

United States | Cross-state surrogacy, parentage and neonatal-care dispute

Dates: US Supreme Court matter 26A325 was docketed on 9 September 2026. Justice Kagan requested a response on 11 September, due by 4 p.m. EDT on 18 September; major-media follow-up was published on 12 September. At publication, the Court has neither granted nor denied a stay and has not decided the merits of parentage, jurisdiction or medical care.

Confirmed by official filings: The applicant seeks to stay a California parentage judgment in proceedings touching California, Texas and Alaska. The application records that the newborn requires a sequence of major heart operations. That context explains the claimed urgency, but the public Supreme Court docket currently records only submissions and the response deadline.

Allegations and party positions: Assertions about demands to terminate the pregnancy, possible interruption of treatment, jurisdiction and service come principally from the applicant's filing. Counsel for the commissioning parties denied to the press that there had been a forced-abortion demand. Neither account is a factual finding by the Supreme Court. This report omits the child's name, image, hospital and other unnecessary identifiers.

System warning

Parentage judgments, medical orders and urgent care may be handled by different courts and institutions. Without a clearly independent representative focused on the child's immediate safety and medical interests, life-changing treatment can become entangled in adult contract and jurisdiction disputes.

Advocacy impact

Adopt a continuity-of-essential-treatment rule, independent child representation, an interstate emergency coordinator and medical-ethics review. Courts should separately answer “who may decide?” and “what does the child need now?” on short deadlines.

Hungary | Government announces plan to change same-sex adoption restrictions

Report date: 12 September 2026. Reuters reported that the government would change restrictions created in 2020. Those rules required single applicants to obtain approval from the family affairs minister and were criticised as an effective barrier to adoption by same-sex couples.

Government position: The prime minister said adoption eligibility should be decided by professionals based on a child's living and developmental conditions, rather than approved case by case by a politician. This is a policy direction, not a published, enacted or commenced law.

To be verified: No bill text, timetable, transitional arrangements, professional criteria or appeals process has been published. The announcement also cannot yet be described as establishing joint adoption rights for same-sex couples; legal effect depends on the eventual text.

System warning

Professional decision-making does not automatically remove bias or opacity. Without published indicators, written reasons, conflict rules and outside appeal, political discretion can simply become administrative discretion.

Advocacy impact

Taiwan's adoption system should also publish waiting times, matching and refusal reasons, how children's views are heard, placement stability and disruption rates. Family assessment should test caregiving capacity, support and the child's individual needs—not substitute identity labels for evidence.

Six immediate audit questions

  1. Is essential treatment protected from interruption while parentage is disputed?
  2. Is an independent person appointed solely to represent the child's best interests?
  3. Does every key decision carry a timestamp, reasons, evidence and a named responsible authority?
  4. Is there one coordinator connecting medical, court and protection information across jurisdictions?
  5. Are adoption eligibility and matching criteria public, appealable and free from irrelevant discrimination?
  6. Are safety, health and placement stability reported—not only application and approval counts?
The child's best interests must be more than a phrase repeated by adults: every decision needs a deadline, evidence, an independent voice for the child and external review.

Legal and reporting boundaries

The US matter currently consists of an emergency application, a response request and conflicting party allegations; the Supreme Court's outcome cannot be predicted. Hungary has announced reform but has not published or changed the law. This report uses the official docket for procedure, major media for party positions, and keeps confirmed facts, allegations, government positions and unresolved points separate.
