What the report says

  • Next TV reported on September 2, 2026 that the Taipei District Court held the first hearing in the case accusing Lin Hsin-tzu of falsifying visit records. The report said she admitted the charged conduct in court and expressed remorse.
  • According to the report's account of the investigation, the disputed records concerned five children, including Kai-Kai. It also referred to a conflict between in-service study and the visit schedule, followed by photographs and records being uploaded later.
  • The report also said a record uploaded on September 26, 2023 did not document the large forehead bruise visible in a photograph taken by a Child Welfare League Foundation social worker the previous day. This is a media account of investigative allegations.

The defense position

Next TV reported that defense counsel argued Lin did make the visit and that the dispute concerned an incorrectly recorded date under the reporting deadline, rather than deliberate fabrication or concealment. Counsel asked the court to consider her remorse and impose a suspended sentence. This is the defense's litigation position, not an independent factual finding by this site.

Keep the child's place visible

The paper layers represent records that were written, altered, and later re-examined. The clay child is an abstract symbol of Kai-Kai. The artwork does not recreate abuse; it keeps attention on the child behind every form—a child who needed to be seen, believed, and protected in time.
