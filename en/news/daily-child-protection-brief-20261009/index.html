DAILY CHILD PROTECTION BRIEF · 2026.10.09

Reporting is only the beginning: is the child actually seen?

From reporting and classification to placement follow-up: examine verifiable safety outcomes and UNICEF’s digital protection appeal.

Today’s verification

This review found no newly confirmed major child-abuse case supported by a court, police, prosecutor or local-government primary release. That is a search limitation, not proof that no abuse occurred. The brief examines existing safeguards and UNICEF’s new 8 October appeal.

Two distinct 24-hour clocks

Evidence A: Article 2 requires designated professionals to report immediately and no later than 24 hours after awareness. Urgent telephone reporting must be followed by the form within that period. Article 5 separately requires the local authority to classify a report within 24 hours of awareness or receipt. The responsible actors and starting points differ. [1]

Article 4 requires appropriate interim protection and immediate medical care when necessary. Our analysis: the deadline must never be treated as permission to leave an endangered child waiting. [1]

Seeing the child

Evidence A: Kaohsiung’s published service description says urgent dangerous cases receive immediate assignment and investigation within 24 hours, with face-to-face contact as the principle. This describes a local service commitment; it does not establish nationwide compliance or a newly amended rule. [2]

Our proposed audit asks why contact was not achieved, what independent safety check replaced it, when another visit occurs and when action escalates. A caregiver’s reassurance alone is not an independently verified outcome.

Placement is a continuing responsibility

Article 9 requires review before emergency placement reaches 72 hours. Where the cause persists and returning home is unsuitable, continued placement may be sought from a court. Article 10 requires extension assessment at least 15 days before expiry and an application, when needed, at least seven days before expiry. Article 11 requires at least one year of follow-up after expiry or revocation. [1]

Articles 8–9 require school and education-authority notification for students. Our analysis asks whether reviews, return-home decisions, school transitions and actual support occur on time. Administrative action and court decisions have distinct procedures. [1]

International update: 8 October

Evidence A: UNICEF Philippines urged broader online protection on 8 October 2026, explaining that age limits or social-media bans alone are unlikely to keep children safe and calling for stronger overall protection systems. [3]

Taiwan implication, as editorial analysis: content reporting should connect with safety assessment, psychological and family support, and verified referral acceptance. This is an international appeal, not a new Taiwanese ban or a finding against a platform.

United Kingdom: implementation remains to be checked

The government’s Crime and Policing Act 2026 factsheet describes sexual-abuse reporting duties for specified child-facing roles and an offence of intentionally obstructing required reporting. Commencement, detailed guidance and measured outcomes need separate verification. [4]

Taiwan already has reporting obligations. The comparison should examine obstruction, delay, response and professional oversight rather than assume that legislation automatically proves effective protection. [1][4]

Five outcomes to publish

Our advocacy proposal is anonymised reporting of receipt-to-classification time, first direct contact and reasons for failed contact, urgent protective action, placement and return-home review timing, and referral plus one-year follow-up completion. Each measure needs a denominator, case types, time period and exceptions.

Outstanding: comparable current data for every county were not obtained. Police data released on 7 August report 10,491 child and youth criminal-case victims in the first half of 2026, down 96 year-on-year. These are criminal-case victims, not an abuse-incidence rate or a protection-effectiveness measure. [5]
