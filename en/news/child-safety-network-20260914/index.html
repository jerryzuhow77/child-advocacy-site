Kaohsiung’s child-safety network: The public question is whether risk was identified in time, whether agencies completed a real handoff, and whether corrective measures can be verified after a major case. After a duty-to-report: a record must become a handoff

Public material in the Kaikai case should be read as one timeline: duty-to-report records, case-management notes, home-visit notes, messages, and follow-up contacts. Cross-checking asks who knew what, who could have verified it, which warning required escalation, and whether unfinished work was visible to the next person.

“Reported” is not a result

Each report needs an owner, deadline, verification method, risk decision, recipient, and open items.

Label evidence levels

Separate formal records, courtroom descriptions, party accounts, media summaries, and unresolved questions.

PROTECTION LENSSystems fail when responsibility is cut into fragments. A handoff must identify the next accountable person, not merely the previous completed action.

The grandmother’s request for help and restricted contact

During placement, adoption, and contact decisions, a relative may not be able to visit freely or independently check a child’s safety. Calling police or asking for help does not automatically restore custody. Those two propositions can coexist: reporting does not automatically restore care, and institutions still have a duty to answer a safety concern.

A responsible intake process should explain what a relative may request, who receives it, the deadline for a safety check, how the outcome is communicated, and where an appeal or review can be made.

PROTECTION LENSWhen a system limits a family’s access and then demands that the family prove the child is safe, protection has been reversed. A traceable response and independent review are essential.

Six questions for the three days before court

  1. Who confirms the child’s actual safety after a report?
  2. When must a case be escalated if risk remains?
  3. Do visit notes record facts, questions, and unresolved risk?
  4. Does every handoff name an owner, deadline, and outcome?
  5. Does a family receive a traceable, reviewable response?
  6. Can every decision be traced to a source, time, and person?
PROTECTION LENSIf a system can answer only whether a report was filed—not whether the child is safe—it has recorded an administrative act, not built a safety net.

Editorial and privacy boundaries
