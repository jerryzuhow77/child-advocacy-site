Home-based care: safety cannot stop at a licence

A proposed rule would include co-residents in safety checks. Its value will depend on fair data, timely visits and a clear route to review.

What the announcement changes

On 20 October, Taiwan’s Social and Family Affairs Administration gave public notice of draft rules on reporting, collecting, querying and using information about unlawful incidents involving childcare personnel, service-centre staff and people who live with a home-based provider. The question is broader than one licence: when care happens at a provider’s registered home, a child encounters the daily environment around that provider.

According to the Administration’s explanation reported by CNA, the 15-article draft would organise information on disqualifying circumstances, work-period sanctions and co-residents, with recurring checks by local authorities. Its reporting duties, penalties and check frequency remain draft design until the consultation and formal notice are complete.

A useful safeguard asks what environment a child can actually enter. It should reduce contact risk without treating family background as a label or turning data checks into unlimited surveillance.

What is confirmed, and what is still pending

Confirmed: the draft has been publicly noticed for 60 days. The Child Care Services Act already contains a framework for responding when a co-resident is implicated in specified risks.

Pending: the final wording, effective date, local implementation rules and staffing capacity. Proposed fines or check intervals should not be described as current obligations before the final order is issued.

Four questions the system must answer

  • How will a move-in, move-out or short-term resident be reported and checked promptly?
  • How can a person correct incorrect cross-agency data and seek review?
  • What safety information can parents receive while protecting privacy and due process?
  • How will visits and decisions leave a traceable record across local authorities and service centres?

What parents can check now

Before arranging care, ask about registration status, the actual care address, people who regularly live in or enter the home, collection arrangements and emergency contacts. Report safety concerns to the local authority or home-based childcare service centre; call 110 or 113 where there is immediate danger.

This briefing summarises an official consultation, the law database and reporting. It does not decide any individual’s eligibility or liability. The aim is safer contact conditions and procedures the public can examine.
