SYSTEM REFORM · 4 SEPTEMBER 2026

Keeping unsuitable personnel out of youth sport

Taiwan's Executive Yuan approved a comprehensive amendment bill that would rename the National Sports Act and place exclusion, registry and safeguarding-training measures on a statutory footing.

Where the bill stands

The Executive Yuan approved the Ministry of Sports's bill on 3 September 2026. It expands the statute from 46 provisions in eight chapters to 69 provisions in twelve chapters and proposes renaming it the National Sports Act. Legislative passage and presidential promulgation are still required.

Four proposed safeguards

  • Exclusion: sexual assault, sexual harassment, sexual bullying and serious corporal punishment may lead to refusal or revocation of certification.
  • Registry: a system would allow checks on unsuitable coaches, referees and sports professionals.
  • Training: people instructing children in sport would complete safeguarding training regularly.
  • Education: schools would supervise normal learning and prevent excessive training and competition; no new elementary-school sports classes would be created.

Proposed commencement

The school-sport chapter is planned for 1 January 2027; the remaining chapters would generally commence on presidential promulgation. Both timing and wording can still change during legislative review.

Why it matters

The bill moves unsuitable-person controls toward statutory, preventive screening. It may offer a model for cross-agency vetting and exclusion in other child-serving settings.

Outside this bill: it does not alter general child-protection risk assessment, reunification thresholds, mandatory-reporting timelines or vertical prosecutorial management of serious abuse cases.

Implementation questions

  • Will checking the registry be mandatory, and what follows if an organization fails to check?
  • Will informal coaches, volunteers, private clubs and short-term camps be covered?
  • How quickly will agencies report and exchange records, and how will errors be challenged?
  • What inspection and measurable training standards will local authorities apply?
