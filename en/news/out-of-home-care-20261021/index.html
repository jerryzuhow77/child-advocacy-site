Placement is not the ending: what helps 746 children stay supported?

On 20 October, New Taipei City said it had placed 746 children and young people through partnerships spanning medical providers, placement services, family-like care and foster families. The service figure matters, but it cannot by itself answer whether every child is safe and steadily supported.

Published 2026.10.21•Event release 2026.10.20Verified public data

Out-of-home placement first removes a child from immediate danger. The next task is to make everyday life stable: school, health and mental-health support, safe ties with important people, and supervision and respite for carers are all part of placement quality.

Five questions that remain after placement

Service volume is a starting point. Safeguarding needs understandable, reviewable processes and outcomes that protect privacy.

  • Are there repeated moves or breaks in transition after placement?
  • Can school, health and mental-health support continue?
  • Are each child’s views and safe relationships respected?
  • Do carers receive sufficient supervision and respite?
  • Are non-identifying processes, timeframes and outcomes made public?

Policy context: placement is an ongoing duty

The city’s release names medical, placement, family-like and foster-care partners, as well as respite for frontline workers. This matters because protection does not end when a child leaves danger; outcomes depend on sustained cooperation across the systems that shape daily life.
