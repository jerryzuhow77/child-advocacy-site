What does this report tell us?

The Ministry of Health and Welfare’s written report of July 30, 2026 states that child-protection reports rose from over 100,000 in 2023 to over 130,000 in 2025. About 90% came through the mandatory-reporting network; within that network, education personnel accounted for 45%, police 21% and social workers 15.2%.

Rules exist; evidence of implementation is missing

The report lists several developments: home-based childcare visits should be unannounced and take place at varying times; abuse outside the home requires handling identity and service-provider information, preserving evidence, checking for other child victims, preventing perpetrators from further contact with children, and notifying parents. From 2026, home-based childcare service centers should also have dedicated supervisors.

However, this is an administrative agency’s account of its own work, not an independent evaluation. Verifiable data and external oversight are still needed to bridge the gap between having rules and implementing them on the frontline.

Turn every commitment into an auditable measure

ReportingPublish reporting rates within 24 hours, reasons for delay and penalties for failure to report.
VisitsPublish the proportions of unannounced visits, visits at varying times and visits where the child was actually seen.
ChecksDisclose when evidence preservation, checks for other affected children and no-contact measures were completed.

The overlooked question: who reviews the decisions?

When frontline staff fail to report, visits do not actually reach the child, or interagency information remains fragmented, retrospective checks by the same agency are insufficient. Authorities should define mandatory review triggers for unusual cases and allow external auditors to examine original visit times, record-edit histories and the timing of notifications across services.

  • Visits without seeing the child, repeated rescheduling or retrospective entries should automatically trigger supervisory review.
  • When one child is harmed in an institution or childcare setting, checks for other affected children should proceed at the same time.
  • Major failures to protect should be followed by de-identified reviews, correction deadlines and published follow-up results.

Next steps: request six sets of data

  1. Reporting-within-24-hours and overdue rates for each mandated profession.
  2. Actual proportions of unannounced, evening and holiday visits in each locality.
  3. Numbers of visits without seeing the child, missed visits, retrospective entries and supervisory reviews.
  4. Time needed to preserve evidence and check for other affected children in abuse outside the home.
  5. Rates of measures barring suspected perpetrators from further contact with children, and responses to breaches.
  6. After dedicated supervisors are assigned: spot-check quality, rates of records returned for correction, and improvement outcomes.
