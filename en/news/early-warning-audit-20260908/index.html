Beyond 70%: bringing early warnings into protection

Read the numbers carefully. Then ask how protection actually reaches a child.

Published / checked: 8 September 2026
01 · Read the denominator 02 · OUR ADVOCACY ANALYSIS 03 · Sources and limits

01 · 46 / 65 = 70.77%

Read the denominator

Audit tables 2–3 cover 65 child deaths from severe intrafamily abuse, neglect or parental murder-suicide in 2022–2024. Of these, 46 (70.77%) had no reporting or service record in the preceding year.

65 Child deaths in the audit group
46 No reporting or service record in the preceding year
19 A reporting or service record in the preceding year

This does not mean no lifetime reports, or prove that someone knowingly failed to report every case.

DETAIL FROM THE AUDIT

“Seventy per cent” is neither one year nor one cause of death

  • The annual shares with no report or service record were 92.86% in 2022, 61.90% in 2023 and 66.67% in 2024. The three-year total is 70.77%; it is not an upward yearly trend.
  • The 65 deaths comprised 27 from severe abuse, 14 from severe neglect and 24 in parental murder-suicide cases. These categories require different routes to early detection.
  • More than four-fifths of deaths from severe abuse or neglect involved children under six; more than half of those killed in parental murder-suicide were aged six to under 18. Prevention cannot depend on children asking for help themselves.
Taiwan National Audit Office | MOHW audit observations, p. 丙－535, tables 2–3 ↗

02 · OUR ADVOCACY ANALYSIS

Every handover needs an answer

The figures expose a gap at the entrance to protection. Reform needs a traceable process. The questions below are our analysis, not verbatim recommendations from the audit.

01

Notice | preserve verifiable information

Which service received the warning? Does its record distinguish direct observation, second-hand information and inference? The next worker needs the source and its limits, not only a risk label.

02

Receive | confirm responsibility

After a referral, who confirms receipt, assessment and next steps? A traceable response prevents “referred” from being mistaken for “safe”.

03

Verify | compare accounts

When caregivers, schools and medical services give inconsistent accounts, is there a clear verification and escalation process? The focus should be the child’s present safety, not making documents agree.

04

Follow up | measure safety

Which risks have decreased, and on what evidence? Agencies can publish anonymised aggregate measures of referral acceptance, assessment and follow-up while protecting children’s privacy.

OFFICIAL RESPONSE

From death review to safe case closure

  • MOHW says it has convened a major child-abuse prevention group and is planning reviews and frontline learning materials based on serious cases.
  • Local authorities have been directed to repeat safety and family-function assessments before closing a child-protection case, and to confirm that the cause of abuse has ended or family functioning has improved.
  • The audit also found some cases were reported again within three months of closure, with roughly 30–40% reopened. “Received services” and “risk resolved” therefore need separate measures.

These are reported official responses and supervisory directions. They do not show that every locality already applies a uniform standard, nor do they establish the deadlines, escalation thresholds or external accountability proposed by this site.
