A safety net must show outcomes,
not just measures

An evidence-layered review of Kaohsiung measures, a campaign proposal and UNICEF research.

Bottom line: publish the time from refused access to in-person safety confirmation, escalation after medical advice, supervisory reversals, and children’s safety outcomes.

Kaohsiung safety-net measures

Media reports attribute online injury consultation, an under-six checklist, supervisory review and police-assisted visits to the city. These are policy statements; primary directives, volumes, response times and missed-case rates remain unverified.

Campaign proposal

Staffing, allowances and counselling are campaign proposals, not enacted budgets or administrative rules. Candidate-supplied figures require checks against official statistics.

UNICEF study

A 21-country estimate covers about 20 million child internet users experiencing online sexual exploitation or abuse in one year; nearly 60% involved social platforms and fewer than 1% formally reported. It cannot be extrapolated directly to Taiwan or the world.
