Reading boundary | A summary is not a judgment

This page is an English summary of the full Traditional Chinese chapter. For investigations, trials and appeals still in progress, it states only the procedural status confirmed in public sources. Administrative findings, professional discipline, civil liability and criminal conviction answer different questions.

Protection chain | Warnings must become action

Reporting, investigation, visits, compulsory entry and protective placement are different stages. An accountable protection chain records who received a warning, who had to verify it, when escalation was required, who accepted the handoff and whether the action was completed.

A job title is not a criminal charge

A case social worker may provide services, visit, document, cross-check and report. A statutory child-protection worker investigates safety and initiates protective measures. Childcare visitors oversee care quality. Supervisors and agencies review decisions and handoffs. Police, prosecutors and courts exercise separate legal powers.

How to read the 24 cases

The full chapter covers 24 cases involving birth families, pre-adoption services, foster care, institutions, reunification, home-based childcare, preschools and cross-jurisdiction transfers. It compares where warnings were lost; it does not impose one liability theory on different facts.

How to compare international cases

The comparison separates seven named cases from one Japanese case-law search result. The absence of an individual criminal conviction does not erase systemic responsibility; an administrative failure alone does not automatically prove negligent homicide.

What changed after the Kaikai case

Confirmed post-case directions include earlier public-authority involvement in adoption-necessity assessment, stronger out-of-home-care handoffs, risk-tiered and unannounced home-childcare visits, and updated childcare legislation. Enactment, commencement and real-world effectiveness must be evaluated separately.

What to do when warning signs appear

If a child faces immediate danger in Taiwan, call 110 or 113; obtain urgent medical care without waiting to collect evidence. Record dates, observations, information sources and the receiving agency. When new warnings appear—such as loss of contact, refused visits or repeated injuries—request a fresh risk assessment.
