Not a scapegoat:
four sets of evidence behind a case-specific duty

This is not about making social workers take the blame. The trial court’s published reasoning connects assigned duties, visits, access to information, and available protective action. Its finding concerning Chen Shang-jie is case-specific—not a judgment against the profession.

What the report gets right

It exposes failures across central government, local government, and the protection network; reform cannot stop at one person.

What needs correction

“No supervision” obscures the fact that contractual, visiting, reporting, and institutional powers existed but were not effectively used.

What must stay distinct

The alleged guarantor duty arose from concrete functions, information, and control—not the occupational title “social worker.”

What the report gets right / what must be challenged

What the report gets right

Drawing on the Control Yuan investigation, NOWnews identifies oversight failures at the ministry and both city governments and highlights structural pressure on frontline social workers. Reform cannot end with one practitioner.

What must be challenged

If “gray zone” and “no supervision” are read to mean that no one held concrete authority in this case, that conflicts with the published first-instance reasoning. System gaps explain how risk grew; they do not erase who held information, who could act, or which protections were not activated.

Chen Shang-jie’s guarantor duty is not the social-work profession

A criminal-law guarantor duty does not arise from a job title alone. It depends on whether the person had a concrete duty to prevent the result. The published first-instance reasoning points to these linked facts:

① Assigned functions
matching, liaison, visits, and follow-up
② Three in-person visits
direct contact and observation of change
③ Information advantage
health, growth, and placement history
④ Available protections
verification, more or unannounced visits, reporting, and seeking a care change
Evidence converging
the case-specific duty found at first instance

System responsibility and individual responsibility can coexist

System level

Ask why inspections, cross-city visits, information sharing, and risk assessment failed.

Case level

Ask what those with information and site access did after warning signs appeared—and which feasible protections were not activated.

This is not liability imposed by job title; multiple items of concrete evidence converge. The first-instance finding rests on assigned functions, actual visits, information access, and available action. It does not impose identical criminal liability on all social workers. Reform the system and examine individual conduct—both are necessary.
