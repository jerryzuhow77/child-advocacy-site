MULTIPLE PERSPECTIVES · CARE CONTINUITY

Separation,
then a final farewell

Caregiver Zhou reportedly said she was willing to continue caring for Kaikai without charge while he waited to be adopted. This was not merely an option on an institutional form, but a familiar caregiving relationship in which the child could feel safe.

THE STORY THAT SHOULD HAVE BEEN HEARD

She was not asking for more resources

Public reporting and court materials describe Zhou as having cared for Kaikai for a substantial period. Reports also state that she was willing to continue caring for him while he waited for adoption. Her hope was that he could grow in a familiar, stable, loving environment.

A young child is not simply placed in a location deemed “qualified.” A familiar caregiver, predictable routines, and consistent, attentive responses are important foundations of felt safety.

What if every available care option had been assessed more carefully, and the child’s safety, stability, and needs had remained the first priority—could the outcome have been different?

The system must leave answers that can be examined

Familiar relationships

Was the child’s attachment to the prior caregiver, daily adjustment, and transition risk assessed?

Available care options

Were continued care, financial support, eligibility pathways, and their limits clearly explained?

How the decision was made

Who compared the arrangements, and were the criteria, exclusion reasons, and response documented for audit?

These questions do not rewrite the outcome after the fact, nor claim that continued care would certainly have prevented the tragedy. They ask child-protection systems to turn the best interests of the child into concrete, reviewable decisions before a major care transition.

Remembering Kaikai means preventing the next missed rescue

Few things are more painful than a separation that becomes a final farewell. Remembering Kaikai is not about remaining in grief; it is about ensuring that children waiting for protection are not missed and left to fall into tragedy.

Evidence boundary: The account of Zhou’s willingness to continue care and join a cooperating system is based on public reporting and court materials. Complete application, eligibility review, financial-assistance, and response records are not public. This is a multiple-perspectives analysis of care stability and the best interests of the child; it does not replace judicial findings.
