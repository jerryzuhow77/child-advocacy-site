FUJIAN · PUTIAN · HISTORICAL CASE

The Fujian Qiqi Case

Waiting for Her Mother at the Village Gate

Before she was twelve, she waited for one person;
after twelve, those left behind waited for an answer.

“Qiqi” is the pseudonym used in public reporting. According to her mother’s retelling of what local residents said, she had sat at the village gate wanting to find her mother; this is not a court finding. Her life should not be reduced to cruel detail. Following four thresholds—the village gate, the school gate, a closed door, and the courtroom—this feature sets out what can be verified and asks why protection did not arrive sooner.

01 · THE WAITVillage Gate

The story of her waiting for her mother comes from family and residents

“Qiqi” is the pseudonym used in public reports. Her biological mother, Ms Bai, said that she learned of her daughter’s death only when the court notified her. She later heard from local residents that Qiqi often sat at the village gate and said she wanted to find her mother.

Family / resident account

“Sitting at the village gate and waiting” is not a fact established by the judgment. It is retained here because the account lets us glimpse a child’s longing, while its place in the evidence is stated plainly.

EDITORIAL QUESTION

When guardianship changes, a parent and child remain out of contact, or a child repeatedly runs away, who is responsible for confirming that she is safe rather than treating her simply as “disobedient”?

02 · SIGNALSSchool Gate

Leaving school, running away, and repeated police reports should not remain isolated records

CCTV.com cited material from the first-instance judgment: in September 2023, a “voluntary withdrawal certificate” recorded Qiqi’s departure from school. Public-security records also showed that, since July 2020, the family had contacted police multiple times for reasons including Qiqi leaving home. These records establish that public systems encountered the situation, but the public record is not sufficient to reconstruct how each contact was handled.

Editorial analysis

Having contact with a case is not the same as seeing the whole picture; completing a single response does not mean the danger has ended. This is a direction for institutional review, not a finding of responsibility against any particular worker.

03 · COURT FINDINGSBehind the Door

The court found that this was not a single loss of control, but sustained harm and acquiescence

At first instance, the Putian Intermediate People’s Court found that the stepmother had subjected three minor family members to long-term abuse. Even after Qiqi had been restrained and confined in a bathroom for seventeen days and had become extremely weak, the harm continued and ultimately caused her death. The forensic finding identified acute circulatory failure caused by multiple factors on a foundation of malnutrition.

Court finding

This page provides only the necessary, non-graphic summary. The public judgments and court notices remain the authoritative sources for the full findings of criminal responsibility.

The father’s criminal responsibility went beyond “failing to stop it”

The first-instance court found that the biological father knew about the long-term abuse but did not stop it, provided items used in and expressed support for the abuse, and had also harmed Qiqi himself when instructed to do so. Within the scope of their joint intent to cause injury, he bore corresponding criminal responsibility for the resulting death. The second-instance court later stated more expressly that, as her legal guardian, he failed in his duty of care, participated in the harm, and condoned and allowed it to continue; the original sentence was “manifestly too lenient.”

Cleaning afterward could not wash away the facts

The judgment materials also recorded actions after the incident, including changing clothing, cleaning the scene, deleting surveillance footage, and asking others to support a story that Qiqi had “fallen.” These details help explain the court’s assessment of culpability and evidence; this page does not reenact those events.

04 · JUSTICEThe Scroll

From five years and six months to thirteen years and six months on retrial

2023.12.22Qiqi died; a criminal investigation began

Emergency personnel noticed signs that something was wrong and notified police. The two defendants were arrested in turn.

2025.04.21Putian Intermediate People’s Court, first instance

Xu Jinhua was convicted of intentional homicide and abuse and received a combined sentence of death. Liu Jiang was convicted of intentional injury and abuse and received a combined prison term of five years and six months.

2025.11.11Fujian High People’s Court, second instance

The appeals were dismissed and the original judgment upheld. The court found Liu Jiang’s sentence for intentional injury manifestly too lenient, but the prohibition against increasing punishment on appeal required a separate adjudication-supervision procedure.

2025.12.23Liu Jiang resentenced on retrial

The sentence for intentional injury was changed to thirteen years; the one-year sentence for abuse was left in place. The combined sentence became thirteen years and six months in prison, with deprivation of political rights for two years.

2026.01.20Death sentence approved and carried out

The Supreme People’s Court approved the death sentence imposed on Xu Jinhua; the Putian court carried it out in accordance with law.

Judicial procedure

The second-instance court did not simply “know the sentence was too light and do nothing.” Bound by the prohibition against increasing punishment on appeal, it upheld the original judgment and then pursued resentencing through an adjudication-supervision retrial. That distinction is essential to understanding the judicial path of this case.

05 · PROTECTIONLeave a Light

Real remembrance joins scattered warning signs into a path to protection

Criminal punishment answers who should bear criminal responsibility, but it cannot replace a child-death review. The institutional questions left by the Qiqi case lie in the gaps between long-term loss of contact, repeated departures from home and police reports, withdrawal from school, a closed caregiving environment, and fragments of information held separately by multiple systems.

01Leaving school is not disappearing

After a minor leaves school, education and child-welfare systems should confirm education, residence, and personal safety—not merely complete a form.

02Read recurring events together

When running away, injury, hunger, fear, or frequent police calls recur, the case should be escalated to a cross-agency risk assessment.

03Check again after a report

A referral does not mean the child is safe. There must be a responsible person, a deadline, and an auditable follow-up check.

04Conduct an independent child-death review

Reconstruct each system’s contact points using de-identified information—not to hunt for scapegoats, but to find where earlier intervention may be possible next time.

Institutional analysis / advocacy

These reforms are proposed by this feature on the basis of public information. They do not mean that a court has found a school, police service, community body, or other agency derelict in its duties.

WHEN A CHILD MAY BE IN DANGER

When warning signs recur, do not carry the final judgment alone

If a child is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services or police. If the danger is not occurring at this moment, you can still make a specific record with local child-welfare, education, or social-service authorities. Note the date, what you witnessed yourself, and the actions already taken. Do not ask the child to repeat their account again and again, and do not confront a suspected abuser yourself.
