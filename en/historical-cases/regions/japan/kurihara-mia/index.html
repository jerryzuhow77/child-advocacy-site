A Japanese child-protection case | 2019 · Noda, Chiba

The Mia Kurihara Case

She had already asked for help.
The adults failed to protect the paper.

A ten-year-old wrote down what she could not safely say at home. Her questionnaire passed through a school, a board of education and a child guidance centre—then a copy was handed to the father she had accused.

“Teacher, can you do something?”
PROLOGUE

The paper travelled through a long corridor

On a winter morning, desks stood in neat rows. Mia used no difficult words. She wrote that she was woken at night, kicked and hit. The paper crossed the institutions meant to protect her, yet returned to the place from which she was trying to escape.

CHAPTER 01

She wrote what she could not say

In November 2017, Mia disclosed her father's violence in a school questionnaire. The school reported it and the Kashiwa Child Guidance Centre placed her in temporary protection. For one moment, the system caught her.

About fifty days later, the pause ended. She went back.

When a child first asks for help, adults must not demand that she prove it again.
CHAPTER 02

Fifty days later, the door closed again

Agencies did not fully integrate their assessments or establish that the danger had passed. Under the father's pressure, the Noda Board of Education gave him a copy of Mia's handwritten questionnaire in January 2018.

A guardian's demand for information cannot outrank a child's safety.
CHAPTER 03

Later words covered the first ones

Mia was later compelled to write that she had not been abused. A withdrawn disclosure does not always mean the danger is gone. Sometimes it means the danger knows she spoke.

CHAPTER 04

Everyone made contact; no one truly saw her

Mia gradually stopped attending school. Calls were answered, documents moved and meetings were held. But reaching a parent is not the same as seeing a child.

The subject of a safety check is the child—not the parent's account.
CHAPTER 05

24 January

The court found that violence had fractured Mia's sternum. In her final days she was deprived of sufficient food and sleep, forced to remain in a bathroom and doused with cold water. She died at home on 24 January 2019. She was ten.

She did not leave without warning. She had already written the danger down.
CHAPTER 06

The court counted responsibility

Her father, Yuichiro Kurihara: sentenced by the Chiba District Court in March 2020 to sixteen years' imprisonment. The sentence became final in March 2021.

Her mother, Nagisa Kurihara: sentenced to two years and six months, suspended for five years with supervision. The court also considered the domestic violence and coercive control she had endured.

A sentence can be counted. The life a child never lived cannot.
CHAPTER 07

The system did not fail all at once

  • Safety was not adequately assessed before temporary protection ended.
  • Risk information was not fully integrated across agencies.
  • The board of education released the child's questionnaire under pressure.
  • Officials did not insist on seeing her directly after prolonged absence.
  • Attention shifted toward responding to the father instead of protecting the child.
QUESTIONS

Five questions that cannot remain blank

  1. Why could protection end after a child clearly asked for help?
  2. Who must safeguard a child's disclosure?
  3. Why can a phone call never replace seeing the child?
  4. How should staff resist a coercive parent?
  5. Who follows the child until safety is real?
鶴

The paper remains. The child does not.

Mia did speak. A child does not need a long journey of paperwork; she needs someone to believe her, stay with her, open the door and not send her back.

When the next child speaks, do not hand her voice back to danger.
