Context and boundaries for reading

Two-year-old boy’s casePublic judgment materials and media reports state that the boy did not receive timely medical assistance after serious injury; the appeal remains pending. Case details, criminal responsibility and sentencing reasons should be determined from judgments and court records.
Chief Liu Zongxin’s caseThe defendant denies murder on appeal and argues that the offence is causing death by bodily injury. This is a litigation argument, not a conclusion; the court must decide on the evidence and legal elements.
Chen Shangjie’s caseProsecutors argue that the sentence and the document-forgery issue should be reconsidered; the defence argues for acquittal. These are matters of adversarial appeal proceedings and remain for the court to decide.

Keep concern within the process

Attending a hearing does not mean reaching a conclusion for either side in advance. It is a way to witness how procedure addresses facts, evidence and responsibility. Do not share information that could identify a minor, mistake a fragment of testimony for a complete record, or treat media reporting or a party’s argument as a court finding.

Entry principles: Arrive early, cooperate with security checks, and observe courtroom order. Do not record audio, video or the hearing. If publishing hearing notes, clearly label them as personal observations rather than an official transcript.
