MULTIPLE PERSPECTIVES · FAMILY PRESERVATION

Should children be supported to remain with their families of origin?

When a child’s safety can be secured, has the system exhausted every reasonable way to let that child grow within familiar relationships, community and daily rhythms?

Our position: Adoption, out-of-home placement and family preservation are not competing answers. Every decision must return to the child’s safety, stability, attachment and long-term well-being.

Support

The conclusion first

Resource allocation affects which options appear feasible.

Adoption

A legal parent-child relationship created through professional matching and court approval; it is not the same as temporary out-of-home care.

Out-of-home placement

Alternative care used to protect safety. Its purpose and duration should be tested against current risk and the possibility of recovery.

Family preservation

Child care, respite, in-home help, financial and parenting support, and intensive visits make safe care at home more than a slogan.

System observation

Commissionable and reimbursable services cannot replace support that is harder to quantify.

Adoption matching may be handled by private service providers licensed by the competent authority and is governed by service procedures, contracts and fee rules. Child-care subsidies mainly help families use public or quasi-public services. These systems are not inherently improper, nor do they establish unlawful profit by any organization or individual.

But if adoption and placement have clear delivery and funding structures while multidisciplinary support for families of origin is fragmented across different offices, eligibility rules and short-term programs, structural incentives may become unbalanced. Safe-at-home plans can then be harder to integrate, activate and monitor over time.

Six questions before separating a child from family

01 | Safety

What is the specific danger? Can medical care, more frequent visits or an adjusted care plan reduce it?

02 | Support

Have child care, financial, housing, mental-health and parenting supports actually been provided?

03 | Relationships

How will the child’s attachment to parents, siblings, grandparents and existing caregivers be maintained?

04 | Review

Who approved the decision not to intensify support? Who reviews whether alternative care remains necessary?

05 | Transparency

Can funding, service volume, referral reasons and outcomes be independently examined?

06 | The child’s voice

In ways suited to age and development, have the child’s feelings and wishes been heard safely?

Data transparency and reform

AreaMinimum data to publish
ResourcesBudgets, service volumes and regional differences across adoption, placement, family preservation and child-care support
DecisionsFamily-support assessments, measures provided, reasons measures were not taken, and supervisory review
QualityVisit frequency, unannounced visits, medical connections and interagency handoffs
OutcomesAnonymized statistics on family reunification, placement changes, adoption and long-term follow-up
  1. Create family-preservation plans with a named responsible person, deadlines and weekly risk reviews.
  2. Use one comparison table to assess safety, attachment, continuity of care, family support and alternative options side by side.
  3. Require independent supervision or multidisciplinary review for major decisions.

Critical boundary: Supporting families of origin does not mean overlooking abuse, serious neglect or immediate danger. When risk cannot be controlled, protection and alternative care must be put in place immediately.
