SOCIAL OBSERVATION · 2026.10.01

2026: Is the nation’s
safety net still safe?

Seven disclosed cases span young children, girls and adults under guardianship. They should not be reduced to one person’s lapse. The harder question is: between reporting, assessment, visits, placement and follow-up, which safeguard failed to close?

ReportingVisitsPlacementRisk reassessmentFollow-up
Scope of this page: This summary draws on public reporting and information made public by the Control Yuan, police, prosecutors or social-welfare authorities. “Suspected” and “alleged” do not establish guilt. The purpose is to identify system points, not to predetermine responsibility in individual cases.

Seven safety-net cases brought into view

01 · Hualien

Two young children died after alleged abuse

High-risk information, including drug and child-abuse reports, was already known, yet gaps emerged in risk assessment, cross-network coordination and searches after loss of contact.

Reporting × lost-contact search
02 · Kaohsiung

One-year-old boy died after alleged abuse by his mother

The child had been reported twice for suspected abuse. The Control Yuan said the city had not fully assessed the risk and did not actively seek police assistance when visits were refused.

Visits × police coordination
03 · Taichung

Two-year-old girl “Xuanxuan” died after alleged abuse

A child-protection case in Tainan, her move exposed a cross-county handoff problem. A change of residence should not interrupt known risks or protective responsibility.

Cross-county handoff
04 · Taipei

Kaikai case: visit records allegedly falsified

After Kaikai’s death, prosecutors charged a Lin-surnamed home-care center visitor in 2026 over alleged falsification of visit records. Whether visits are real and auditable is fundamental to protection.

Record integrity × auditability
05 · Taipei

Ten-year-old girl: alleged sexual molestation and a 240-day placement dispute

After the allegation, judicial protective placement was not completed for 240 days. The Social Affairs Bureau said it continued visits and risk assessment before reassessing and starting placement.

Placement timing × reassessment
06 · Taipei

Former social-work supervisor alleged to have embezzled more than NT$12 million from older adults

A former supervisor was alleged to have used access to accounts of adults under guardianship to embezzle funds. Prosecutors sought detention, and the court ordered detention without communication.

Guardianship × financial oversight
07 · New Taipei

Girl made to walk in public wearing a humiliating sign

The family was reported for improper discipline and received a protection order in 2025; a public-humiliation incident occurred again in 2026. Risk must continue to be tested after an order and course completion.

Protection order × follow-up

The question is not only whether someone entered the system, but whether the system kept seeing them

Entry into a protection system does not mean risk has disappeared. A safety net needs a trace that can be reviewed, handed over and escalated: who received the information, when a visit occurred, what followed a refused visit, who took over after a move, and what the safety plan was before placement. Every renewed signal requires a new assessment.
