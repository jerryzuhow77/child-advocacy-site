When warning signs of tooth loss recur, the question is not only whether a single consultation was enough to recognize risk, but also whether those with a longer service history verified, followed up, escalated and handed over the information.

Scope of this account:This article draws on public hearings, testimony and statements by the people involved solely to examine systemic issues in risk reporting and service follow-up. Each person's legal responsibility remains subject to the court's final determination.

The real question is not about assigning all responsibility to one role

Whether the dentist should have reported is open to discussion. At the same time, scrutiny cannot bypass staff who held information over time, could contact the caregiver and family, and could activate internal organizational support and coordination across systems.

  • Were repeated or not reasonably explainable signs of injury fully recorded?
  • Was medical advice verified, followed up and reported to supervisors?
  • Was the family informed in time and given an opportunity to provide information or help?
  • When concerns increased, was protective action taken under the law, organizational rules and professional judgment?

“The caregiver deceived us” is not an answer that ends scrutiny.In the child safety net, what matters is whether every warning is rechecked and escalated until the child receives effective protection.
