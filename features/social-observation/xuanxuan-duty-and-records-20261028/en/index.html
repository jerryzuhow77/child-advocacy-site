DIVERSE PERSPECTIVES · 28 OCT 2026 · VERIFIED 29 OCT

The Xuanxuan case: who was charged, and who was disciplined?

Different people and different procedures cannot be collapsed into one claim. What public materials currently establish: the mother was charged on suspicion of negligent homicide; the Tainan caseworker received professional discipline. No public prosecutorial or court document has been found showing that the caseworker was charged with negligent homicide.

BOTTOM LINE

“The social worker was charged with negligent homicide” is not an established fact at present

June reporting says prosecutors charged the mother’s cohabiting partner on suspicion of causing death through abuse and charged the mother on suspicion of negligent homicide. The October public record concerns the Tainan worker’s licence and practice certificate being revoked over visits, records and false visit information. Both are serious, but the responsible parties and procedures are different.

ESTABLISHED · THREE TRACKS

Separating the tracks makes responsibility legible

01 · Criminal charge

Public reporting identifies the cohabiting partner and the mother as defendants: alleged fatal abuse and alleged negligent homicide respectively. An October 21 report says the man’s detention was extended; court proceedings remain ongoing.

02 · Professional discipline

The Tainan caseworker was found not to have carried out required visits or records, and to have supplied false visit information. Her social-worker certificate and practice licence were revoked.

03 · Administrative and systems review

Who carried the case after relocation, how Taichung handled a report, and how cross-city information moved are safety-net questions. They are not a criminal finding against any person or agency.

LEGAL ANALYSIS · NOT A SUBSTITUTE FOR EVIDENCE

Why does professional misconduct not automatically become negligent homicide?

Criminal liability requires each element

A specific duty of care, breach, causation, foreseeability and the death outcome must each be proved. A work failure or professional sanction cannot simply skip those steps.

“Guarantor status” is not automatic for social workers

MOHW’s April 2026 cross-disciplinary statement says such status is case-specific; even where it may exist, it does not automatically produce criminal liability.

This is a general description of legal structure, not advice or a finding of guilt about anyone in this case.
